**The Low Republic: A History of the Galaxy, 19 BBY – 2 ABY**, by Pablito and HyeroDrimm, is the history this wiki is built from. It is given here chapter by chapter, with the authors' corrections. Each chapter or section names the article that covers it. The biographical register, Chapter XIV, is not carried here: each figure in it has an article of their own.

## Preface

The name was coined by Coruscanti journalists during the reform years. The people it described adopted it without much protest. It was meant as a contrast with the High Republic of several centuries earlier, that confident, expanding, brightly-lit age the Jedi still taught to their younglings. What came after Palpatine was poorer in certainty and smaller in ambition. It was also permanently conscious of how narrowly it had survived. Several of the people who wrote the new charter used the term themselves in debate.

A new galactic epoch was pronounced later, and the calendar was reset not by the Republic's own wishes.

The following Star Wars media is considered fully canon to the Low Republic AU:

- Episode I: The Phantom Menace
- Episode II: Attack of the Clones
- Episode III: Revenge of the Sith (up until the scene of Palpatine being cornered by Windu)
- the entirety of the Star Wars: The Clone Wars animated series
- Star Wars: Tales of the Jedi (Dooku episodes)

All other media are to be assumed as non-canon to this AU in their entirety. If some elements of other Star Wars media are explicitly mentioned in the document, then those elements are considered canon, but the entirety of the media those elements originate from is not necessarily canon. For instance, the Voss, who originate from the Old Republic era, are canon to the Low Republic, but that does not mean that the entirety of the Old Republic is canon.

## I. The Death of Palpatine

*Main article: [[The Death of Palpatine]]*

### The Chancellor's office, 19 BBY

Four Jedi entered the Supreme Chancellor's private office to arrest him on suspicion of being a Sith Lord. Agen Kolar, Saesee Tiin and Kit Fisto were dead within nine seconds. Mace Windu duelled Palpatine alone, disarmed him, and forced him against the window.

Anakin Skywalker arrived. He did not intervene. Windu struck.

Skywalker gave his account many times over the following decades and never changed it. He described a failure to decide in time, not any noble conviction. Palpatine had promised him knowledge that would prevent his wife's death in childbirth. That promise was extinguished in front of him. Padmé Amidala survived the birth of twins four months later. Whether the danger or the promise to avoid it had ever been genuine was never established, but Skywalker did not stop considering the question.

### The limits of what he knew

Skywalker's testimony destroyed Palpatine's reputation within a week. It did not dismantle what Palpatine had built, because Skywalker did not know its full shape.

He could establish that Palpatine was Darth Sidious, that Dooku had been his apprentice, and that the Separatist crisis had been steered from both ends. He knew nothing else. Not the Kaminoan contingency orders. Not the Sifo-Dyas commission. Not the Sith shrine beneath the Jedi Temple. Not the name of a single one of Palpatine's people outside the Chancellery. Those were found later by accountants, and the finding of them shaped the age more than the killing had.

### The first weeks

Mace Windu presented himself at the Judiciary Building at dawn and handed over his lightsaber. He appears to have considered this an obvious duty rather than a political act. Any other course of action, in his mind, would've resulted in open conflict between the Jedi and the Republic. What he did settled an important question before anyone could ask it. The Jedi would answer to ordinary courts like anyone else. The Order never managed to take that back.

Vice Chair Mas Amedda took over as acting Chancellor. He spent his six weeks in the post bargaining for his own protection from prosecution. He did it with such patient self-interest that he held up the business of replacing Palpatine by two months.

For eleven hours on the second night, the Coruscant Guard ringed the Jedi Temple while the Senate argued about whether it had just watched a coup. A later inquiry established that the Guard's commander had received no orders of any kind. He had acted on his own initiative to make sure nobody would try to escalate the situation. Palpatine's body showed that the dark side had worked itself into him over many years. A red lightsaber was recovered at the scene. Roughly a third of the galactic population never accepted any of it, and still does not. Most of them never saw a Jedi in their life, but Palpatine's face and promises were on every holoscreen.

### The escape of Maul

Four days before the killing, the Zabrak warlord Maul had been captured on Mandalore by Commander Ahsoka Tano and the 501st Legion. He was put aboard a Venator-class Star Destroyer bound for Coruscant.

He was the only prisoner in the galaxy who understood immediately what Palpatine's death meant. He had known his former master's identity for over a decade and had been dismissed as a lunatic every time he said so. When the news of Palpatine's death reached the fleet, its chain of command was in disarray. There were recall orders, competing authorities, rumours of a Jedi coup, and a Senate demanding the return of every capital ship in the Core. Ahsoka was temporarily disarmed and detained. Maul escaped in the confusion, tearing apart half of the Star Destroyer in the process, and reached Mandalorian space within the month. When life support started failing, Tano was released from confinement, but prioritised saving lives over pursuing Maul. She does not regret her choice, but still views the operation as one of the greatest failures of her life.

## II. The Long War, 19–15 BBY

*Main article: [[The Long War]]*

### Why it continued

The Confederacy of Independent Systems was not defeated in 19 BBY. It was beheaded. Dooku was dead. Grievous was killed on Utapau within hours of Palpatine. But the Separatist Council survived on Mustafar. The droid armies were still taking orders from regional commanders. The Confederacy still held some fifteen hundred member systems, along with the factories of the Techno Union, the Trade Federation, the InterGalactic Banking Clan, the Commerce Guild and the Corporate Alliance.

Nobody had the standing to surrender on its behalf. So the Separatists did not surrender. They broke into two dozen regional commands and fought on for four more years. A war against two dozen enemies proved considerably more draining to finish than a war against one. The Clone Wars have ended, and the Long War has begun. It was called as such not because it was much longer than the Clone Wars. Rather, it came directly after the previous conflict, with no break in between, making the galactic populace feel the length of the war much more profoundly.

### The double revelation

Proof that Dooku had served Sidious hit the Confederacy in two directions at once. It knocked the legs out from under the whole war. The Confederacy's founder had been the servant of the Chancellor he claimed to oppose. Separatist morale collapsed across hundreds of worlds within a year. It also proved the Confederacy's original complaint far more thoroughly than anyone in the Confederacy had ever dared claim. The Republic had not merely been corrupt and bought by the great corporations. It had been run by a Sith Lord for thirteen years and nobody in it had noticed.

What this meant in practice was that the partisan fronts wanted to go home and their leaders could not. Peace meant trials and seized property for every executive who had signed a Confederate charter. The war's final years were fought largely by corporate boards with private armies. They were the only people left with nothing at all to gain from stopping.

### The Concord of Raxus, 15 BBY

The war ended in a bargain rather than a surrender. Lux Bonteri led the negotiations, because he was the only person there willing to acknowledge Separatist misgivings as legitimate. His mother had been murdered for arguing for exactly this. Under the Concord, systems coming back into the Republic kept the right to run their own affairs. The great corporations lost their Senate seats for good. Soldiers below command rank were pardoned. The Confederate leadership went on trial. And the droid armies were to be broken up and scrapped, with the Republic overseeing the work.

The promises of self-government were kept, but taxes were still collected. The scrapping was never thoroughly finished. Nute Gunray and Rune Haako were tried and convicted in 14 BBY. Shu Mai vanished in 18 BBY with a great deal of the Commerce Guild's ready money and was never found.

## III. The Kamino Findings and the Reconstitution

### The audit

*Main article: [[The Kamino Findings]]*

The investigation into Palpatine was exhaustive and it was carried out by accountants. Every contract, payment and appointment made under his authority across thirteen years was opened up again. The largest single item in the Republic's history was the Kaminoan clone contract. It had been ordered in the name of the murdered Jedi Master Sifo-Dyas and run after his death by Dooku.

Republic auditors reached Kamino in 18 BBY with the power to demand any record they wanted. Jedi Master Shaak Ti went with them. She had overseen clone production for three years, and in 21 BBY she had been assured that the trooper Fives was suffering from a tumour, which caused a psychotic episode. The Kaminoans had broken no law of their own. They kept meticulous paperwork proving they had followed their client's instructions exactly, and they produced those instructions.

### The contingency orders

*Main article: [[The Kamino Findings]]*

Every clone trooper carried a small organic chip put in during growth, holding one hundred and fifty standing orders. Order 66 told the troops to kill every Jedi officer over them. At once, on a single transmission, with no confirmation and no appeal.

The finding was published within six days over the objections of the Ministry of Defence and without their approval. To this day, nobody has been able to establish who gave the order to release the evidence to the public. Kamino findings went a long way towards restoring the Jedi in the public mind, since the Order had been one signal away from extinction. It arrived during Windu's trial, where it did more for his defence than anything his lawyers managed. It also settled one thing permanently. The Grand Army of the Republic had been a Sith weapon built to a Sith design, and no amount of surgery would make it anything else. What it also meant is that many people in the galaxy would never trust a clone again.

Fives was cleared posthumously in 17 BBY. Nobody was punished, since every officer involved in his death had been acting on a faked medical report.

### The Reconstitution, 18–12 BBY

*Main article: [[The Manumission Act]]*

The chips came out between 18 and 17 BBY and every surviving trooper was cleared. Production was stopped in 17 BBY and the Kaminoan contract was cancelled the same year.

The Manumission Act of 16 BBY made every clone a citizen. Padmé Amidala drafted it and Bail Organa carried it. Ahsoka Tano and Captain Rex argued it through the Senate, giving more than four hundred hours of testimony between them. It passed as much out of necessity as conscience. Eight million living beings could not be released from service into nothing at all, with no standing under any law. Two million of them were children. About half of the adults signed on again as volunteers. They served in the Auxiliary Corps, beside conscripted citizens who had been given a few weeks of training. The clones fought the rest of the Long War as citizens of a Republic they had already defended for seven years without belonging to it. Most of them died before it ended. A little over three million adult clones were left alive when the Long War was over.

Beside them the Republic began raising a recruited army in 18 BBY, the first since the Ruusan Reformations. It was called the Republic Corps, and it is described in Section XII. The last Auxiliary regiment stood down in 12 BBY.

### The ones still growing

*Main article: [[The Manumission Act]]*

Production of clone batches stopped in 17 BBY, but a clone takes ten years to reach deployment age. Everyone decanted after 27 BBY was still growing when the contract was cancelled. There were about two million of them, and the youngest were only days old.

The Republic became their guardian overnight and had no plan for it. Although the main production contract was cancelled, the Kaminoans were kept on under a stripped-down agreement. Accusations and allegations about the immorality of their cloning practices were quietly dropped, in exchange for the Kaminoans taking on the duty to see the underaged clones to adulthood, because they already had the facilities and know-how to raise them. The work was done at cost and without enthusiasm on either side. A Ministry of the Interior programme advertised the children for adoption across the Core and the Inner Rim. It ran for nine years, and out of the pool of two million, sixty thousand were adopted by independent families, fifty thousand were adopted by adult clone troopers, and three thousand were adopted directly by the Jedi Order when they moved. Some of the latter decided to establish the Temple Trooper regiment and still serve on Ossus.

Of course, this entire clone cohort was not a group with consistent age. The oldest underwent cadet training before production was stopped, so they were soldiers who were never deployed. Those decanted after 21 BBY were raised as wards and have never received accelerated combat training. All of them share a face and nothing else, aside from the distaste towards the Republic. There were also clone embryos still in the tanks in 17 BBY, but the Republic has never published what happened to them.

### What a recruited army changed

*Main article: [[The Republic Corps]]*

A permanent army under civilian control created something that had not existed in 22 BBY: a permanent military interest. Shipyards with contracts. Veterans' associations. Garrisons in vital sectors and along trade lanes. A general staff with opinions about policy. All of it proved lasting.

More importantly, the dead came home. Clone casualties had been a line in a budget. Recruited casualties were somebody's constituents. They went back to their home worlds in numbered caskets and were mourned by voters who had a senator.

This is the main reason the Long War ended when it did. The Separatist remnants were not beaten. By 15 BBY the voters would no longer pay to beat them. The last holdout commands were bought off, bribed, or quietly given the self-rule they had spent seven years fighting for.

### The abandonment

*Main article: [[The Manumission Act]]*

The clones had grown fast and they aged fast to match. Troopers decanted in 32 BBY were physically thirty-six by 14 BBY, and fifty-six in 4 BBY. Citizenship arrived about a decade too late to be much use to the men who had won the war without it.

Their pensions had been worked out on the assumption that they would age like ordinary men. They were not corrected until 6 BBY, by which point most of the men they were meant for were dead. Laws to protect clone veterans looking for work were debated in four sessions running and passed in none of them.

The Manumission Act put about six million men out of service in a single year, even if half of them re-enlisted. Those that survived the Long War were dismissed gradually as their formations stood down, until the last in 12 BBY. Every one of them was physically old and legally brand new. They were trained for one job that no longer existed, distrusted by most of the galaxy, and drawing a pension calculated for a man of thirty. They were the largest pool of unattached military skill in the galaxy, and somebody was going to hire them.

Around one and a half million of the eight million are still alive in 2 ABY. Most of them never fought. The men who served were those decanted before 27 BBY and most are physically approaching seventy. The ones raised as wards are physically between forty and sixty. The latter are the reason the survivor numbers are as high as they are. Eighty-six thousand clones are still on Collective payroll, which is where the medical care is. After the pension correction passed in 6 BBY, to account for clone ageing, the clones that were still alive at least got enough funds to settle down and live out the rest of their days on some humble planet in the Mid or Outer Rim. Slowing clone ageing has been possible for years but the Republic has never sponsored it. A man who ages twice as fast draws a pension for half as long.

## IV. Tarkin and the Shadow Bargain

*Main article: [[Wilhuff Tarkin]]*

### The ascent

Wilhuff Tarkin had been Palpatine's protégé but was not part of his conspiracy, having been a naval officer rather than a politician. He cooperated with the investigation immediately and completely, to the obvious ruin of several men who had thought of him as a friend. The investigators found him invaluable. It was the first of many occasions on which his usefulness outvalued anyone's willingness to look at him closely.

The Long War was his ladder. By 17 BBY he held the Outland Command. It was a theatre posting in the Rim with unusually wide freedom over supply, over who answered to whom, and over what happened to captured Separatist property. He used it to build something no Republic officer had held in a thousand years: a personal following in the officer corps, and a group of sectors that looked to him before they looked to Coruscant.

### The bargain

Tarkin made contact with Maul in 17 BBY. The earliest surviving record is a supply manifest from that year, and the arrangement was almost certainly older than the paperwork.

The deal was simple, as far as anyone could piece it together afterwards. Maul's people killed for Tarkin. Separatist field commanders, the bankers paying for them, and any Republic official standing in Tarkin's way. In exchange Tarkin gave Maul intelligence, safe passage, three sectors where nobody would come looking, and a share of captured Separatist wealth that never reached Coruscant.

What made it work was that none of the killings looked like what they were. Separatist commanders appeared to have been purged by their own side. The bankers appeared to have been dealt with by syndicates they owed money to. And the Republic officials appeared to be victims of Separatist terror. A magistrate on Ord Mantell died in 16 BBY while examining the Outland Command accounts. Two admirals who had asked about the same accounts died within the year. Senator Fang Zar of Sern Prime was shot in a transit tunnel in 15 BBY. He was due to give evidence the following week about where the captured Separatist property had gone.

Tarkin never gave an order anyone could follow back to him. He described a problem in front of the right person and let them reach their own conclusion. He seems to have understood from the start that what Maul wanted out of the arrangement was not money but leverage. He decided that was a price worth paying. And to a large extent, he was right.

### What it bought

By the Concord of Raxus, Tarkin was the most decorated commander in the Republic. He ran a group of Rim sectors in everything but name and had outlived every rival who had seriously got in his way. He had gathered nearly as much power as a victorious general might have taken by force. And he got all of it through channels that were, taken one at a time, entirely lawful.

The bargain ended in 1 BBY and neither man has moved against the other since. Quelii and Seswenna sit at opposite ends of the Rim. Beyond that, each is useful to the other. Prosperous Rim sectors are the Collective's best argument that the Republic is not needed. A Republic coming apart is Seswenna's best argument that local authority is the best choice.

## V. The Republic Remade

### Administrations

*Main article: [[The Republic Remade]]*

- 19 BBY (six weeks): Mas Amedda, acting
- 19–14 BBY: Bail Organa
- 14–11 BBY: Ars Vehn
- 11 BBY – 0 ABY: Mon Mothma
- 0 ABY – present: Sevrin Dal

Organa governed through the war's continuation, the trials and the Kamino findings. He stepped down exhausted, having pleased nobody. Vehn, a former sector admiral, won on public exhaustion and governed as the Security faction's man. Mon Mothma was the first Chancellor to serve under the new charter. Dal was elected six weeks after Yavin on an open promise of revenge.

A Chancellor can be removed by the Senate on a two-thirds vote. Vehn is the only one it has ever managed. Three motions have been filed against Dal in two years. The best of them reached forty-one percent. All three came from the Rim self-rule bloc, and all three raised the number of sectors thinking about leaving.

### The Constitutional Convention, 13–11 BBY

*Main article: [[The Republic Remade]]*

It met under a Security government, which is the main reason it produced rules with teeth instead of a statement of good intentions. Padmé Amidala chaired it. Through it all, she had to fight from the senatorial seat of Naboo, a place that has been more scorned by the day, a place that produced Palpatine and Binks. A Sith Lord who nearly destroyed the Republic and a fool who made it possible due to his own idiocy. Add to it the inquiry into her husband's conduct at Murkhana, and all this has put the Chancellorship permanently out of her reach. So she ended up writing the rules that other people would govern under.

What it achieved: emergency powers now expired automatically after ninety days unless the Senate voted to keep them, with no exceptions written in for any reason. Corporations were barred from holding Senate seats. The army and navy became permanent and answered to a civilian ministry rather than to the Chancellor. The courts were put out of the Chancellor's reach. And a standing Investigative Office was set up with the power to demand any record the government held.

What it failed at: giving the Outer Rim more seats, which the Core worlds voted down; getting rid of appointed sector governors; freeing the Jedi from the ordinary courts; and writing any law against slavery that could actually be enforced in or near Hutt space. The Convention also chose to say nothing whatsoever about how a sector might leave the Republic. The argument that won was that writing down a way out is an invitation to use it. So nothing was written down, keeping the status quo of no legal way to secede from the Republic. This turned out to prevent nothing.

### The Geonosis Programme

*Main article: [[The Republic Remade]]*

Superweapon plans of Geonosian design, recovered during the occupation in 18 BBY, became a secret programme under the Ministry of Security, paid for out of a budget line marked planetary defence. The new Investigative Office exposed it in 11 BBY at roughly eleven percent built. Vehn was immediately impeached. The programme's director, Orson Krennic, was demoted and reassigned to Outland Command. The hull was scrapped. The plans and some Geonosian-made components were never recovered.

### Seswenna

*Main article: [[The Greater Seswenna Union]]*

Tarkin resigned his commission in 10 BBY, went home to Eriadu, and was elected governor of the Seswenna sector. Over the following decade he drew the neighbouring frontier sectors into an efficient, prosperous and orderly bloc. Elections went on being held and stopped being competitive. He kept being re-elected term after term, through an overwhelming majority. Coruscant put up with the arrangement because Seswenna kept the shipping lanes safe, paid what it owed, and cleaned up its own droid remnants without asking for Republic troops. Director Wullf Yularen of the reformed Strategic Information Service, known to everyone as Republic Intelligence or SIS, opened a file on the Outland Command accounts in 11 BBY and did not close it for nine years.

### What was not repaired

*Main article: [[The Republic Remade]]*

The Trade Federation lost its Senate seats and reappeared within four years as three separate companies with the same men on the board. Corruption in the sectors carried on. The courts were slower in 1 BBY than they had been in 19 BBY. The Outer Rim was still short of seats and short of policing, and still right in believing that the Core never thought about it. And then there were the droid papers. Between 21 and 15 BBY at least nine independent studies concluded that many Separatist droids displayed sentience and in some cases came out of the factories with faculties that bordered on self-awareness. Four of the nine studies were funded by Republic universities, and each one was buried. Two researchers lost their funding within a month. One was prosecuted for mishandling classified material, and the charge was quietly dropped after the war.

The Investigative Office published the lot in 6 BBY, together with the memoranda showing who had ordered them suppressed. The reasoning in those memoranda was not complicated. The Republic was about to destroy tens of millions of those things and did not want a debate about it first. Nothing has been done with this. There is no committee, no proposal and no law. The question of what exactly was scrapped under the Concord of Raxus is regarded on Coruscant as settled, in the sense that nobody will raise it.

### The Kashyyyk Region Coalition, 14 BBY

*Main article: [[The Kashyyyk Region Coalition]]*

The Long War was fought hardest in the Mid Rim. The Taldot sector lost most of its industry and fourteen of its worlds were left completely uninhabitable. The damage spread into the Mytaranor, Lantillian, Halla and Kastolar sectors. Kashyyyk had been held at enormous cost in 19 BBY and was not rebuilt either. No reconstruction money came, because all of it was going into government reforms and the recruited army. Halla and Kastolar border Hutt Space, and the Hutts moved in while the sectors were still counting their dead. Nal Kaddan fell in 15 BBY. Both sectors begged for help. Nothing came out of Coruscant except condolences and empty promises.

The Governor of Taldot called the other four together in 14 BBY. They agreed to pool what was left. Shipping, yards, food, and whatever militia each of them could still raise. Kashyyyk brought most of the latter. The Wookiees had been fighting Trandoshan slavers for centuries without help, and they were eager to strike back at the Hutts. It took two years to push the Hutts back out of Halla and Kastolar. Then they all went to the Senate and asked for their taxes to be cut to a fraction. They got it, because the Perlemian Trade Route runs through their territories and the Senate could not risk them blockading it. The Coalition became official.

The five sectors have been rebuilt since. They are richer now than they were before the war. They have a combined military force on standby that nobody around them wants to test. The Wookiees have never shown anyone their secret hyperspace routes, but they navigate Coalition ships through them for efficient intra-region travel. None of the five sectors has ever filed any complaint to the Senate. No other sector bloc has been offered the same special treatment and the Ministry has found plenty of excuses to not give anyone else the same deal. In reality, the difference is that the others do not have a trade route that the Republic needs kept open.

### The census, 4 BBY

*Main article: [[The Census of 4 BBY]]*

The Convention ordered the first full census of the Republic in four centuries. With a galactic scale to work with, it took six years to finish. The Ministry of the Interior counted a little over eighty billion people in over a thousand sectors. That figure surprised many, but was logical in the end.

To measure population change, the census needed older records. The Republic had none that reached back more than a few centuries, but the Jedi did. For over a thousand years the Order had blood-tested newborns on member worlds, looking for Force-sensitive children, and it had kept every result. Investigators had already been through those archives in 18 BBY, looking for traces of Sith manipulation. Nobody had considered them as a count of births until the census office asked.

The records confirmed something that everyone already accepted as fact and never brought up in daily life. Most habitable worlds lay abandoned, the ruins littering them being the stuff of legends for centuries now. Families are small and children are difficult to conceive. A household with three children is the talk of the town. What the records have shown is how long that had been true. A thousand years ago, during the Republic Dark Age, the Candorian plague ravaged the galaxy. It killed two-thirds of the entire galactic population. Those who survived had fewer children, and so did their descendants. Every generation since has been smaller than the one before. Before the plague, the same worlds that now house billions or lie barren used to hold hundreds of trillions of lives, with Coruscant alone supporting three trillion lives.

What the census also found is that the population decline had stopped. Sometime in the last few decades, each generation began to replace itself again. People of the Republic are still mostly old, so the dead will outnumber the newborn for another thirty years, but after that the population should hold steady, for the first time in a thousand years.

The report was published in 4 BBY, and it caused quite a stir, though not anything close to the outrage that the Kamino findings provoked. Small families had never been thought of as a condition; it was simply how things were. Now it had a beginning, and an end in sight.

## VI. The Reckoning of the Jedi

*Main article: [[The Reckoning of the Jedi]]*

### The Windu trial, 19–17 BBY

Two years, and the most closely watched trial in Republic history. Windu was acquitted on the grounds that the threat had been immediate. The defence rested on three dead Jedi, the evidence from Palpatine's chambers, Skywalker's testimony, and in the final year the Kamino findings. The verdict was sound and changed few minds. What lasted was the precedent, and Windu had set it himself on the first morning without meaning to. He resigned from the Council in 17 BBY and became a teacher and instructor at Ossus after the Order moved there.

### The shrine, 18 BBY

Digging beneath the Jedi Temple turned up an ancient Sith shrine over which the Temple had been deliberately built. The Council chamber stood directly above it. The Order had a little over ten thousand members when the Clone Wars began, and close to two thousand of them died in the two wars. Five hundred Jedi resigned within eighteen months. The question that drove them out was not why the Order had failed to spot Sidious; that was already obvious. It was what the Order had been meditating on top of for a thousand years.

### Demilitarisation, 16 BBY

Jedi military rank was abolished by agreement between the Council and the Senate. In practice this followed from the Reconstitution rather than from Jedi faults. Clones were being dismissed, and the new recruited force had no place in it for generals who took orders from a religious cult. Some of the Jedi that left the Order joined the army again as civilians. The Order gave up command in the middle of a war it had been fighting for six years. Several thousand Jedi were called home from places where they had spent those years defending particular people who had come to rely on them. They had to explain to those people why they were leaving. A good many never forgave the Council for it. The resentment left behind by that one administrative decision did the Order more damage over the following two decades than the Sith conspiracy had.

### The move to Ossus, 15 BBY

The Temple on Coruscant had been an excavation since 18 BBY. Investigators had been through every level of it. The shrine underneath had been opened, catalogued, photographed and argued about in the Senate. The Jedi Archives were turned upside down for any trace of Sith manipulation. The Council voted to leave in 15 BBY and the move took two years. The Order went to Ossus. It had been a Jedi world when the Republic was young, it held the ruins of the old library, and it had the considerable advantage of belonging to nobody. The Temple was left standing empty under Republic caretakership, and it stayed empty for thirteen years.

As the Jedi left Coruscant, they left an open invitation for all clone veterans to join them. Several thousand clones went immediately, and it's estimated that over the years Ossus has been a place where over eighty thousand clones lived out the rest of their days. By 2 ABY, around twenty thousand are still alive, albeit most elderly. Some started families, as several frontier villages sprang up on Ossus, at a respectful distance from the Jedi Temple. Shaak Ti is a liaison between the Temple and clone communities in those settlements, personally overseeing the wellbeing of remaining clones and doing her best to prolong their time with Force rituals. She has seen moderate success at it, though she did determine that children of clones are perfectly healthy and do not suffer from accelerated ageing or other health complications.

### The survivors

Because Order 66 was never sent, the Order kept a generation of senior masters who would have otherwise died within an hour of one another. Plo Koon, Ki-Adi-Mundi, Aayla Secura, Shaak Ti, Luminara Unduli, Stass Allie and Coleman Kcaj all came through the Long War. The preserved knowledge and continuity of leadership made the Order's restructuring smoother than it could've otherwise been. None of it prevented what followed.

### The schism, 14–8 BBY

The Skywalker marriage became public in 19 BBY, Amidala having been visibly pregnant all through her husband's testimony. The Council refused to make an exception. He left the Order in 17 BBY. He was then immediately re-hired as a civilian and made general in the new Republic Corps. He was still one of the most effective and talented commanders in the Core and was more than willing to continue the fight.

The wider question could not be dealt with so easily, and three camps emerged. The reformists held that forbidding attachment had produced Dooku and had very nearly corrupted Skywalker. Bonds you forbid do not stop existing, they argued. They simply stop being supervised. The traditionalists held that Skywalker proved the rule rather than broke it, and that the real mistake had been years of treating him like a favourite student and looking the other way for his misbehaviours. The withdrawalists held that both camps were treating symptoms. The Order's true error had been to serve a government at all, to create rules and regulations on how to feel and what to think, instead of rejecting ephemeral structures and following only the will of the Force.

Yoda moved towards the reformists over five years. His argument was that the Order's rigidity had been a worse failure than any particular teaching.

The Ossus Compact of 8 BBY settled matters partly, between the first two camps. Younglings could keep contact with their birth families. Openly acknowledged bonds were permitted. Possessive attachment was still forbidden. Where exactly the line fell was left to be judged case by case, which meant it was left to whoever happened to be judging.

The withdrawalists lost outright and did not argue further. Six hundred and thirty-two Jedi resigned in the eleven weeks after the Compact. They gave up their rank, their access, and their lightsabers, and scattered without saying where they were going. Among them was Master Aayla Secura. She had spent six years attached to the men she commanded and had been ordered to leave them. She came out of that decision agreeing with every word the withdrawalists said. A quarter of all the Jedi that survived the war left gradually over the whole reform period, not including the withdrawalist separation. Some became teachers, farmers and freight pilots. Some eventually found their way to Dathomir. The withdrawalists went somewhere else entirely, and it was five years before anyone found out where.

## VII. The Shadow Collective

### What it is

*Main article: [[The Shadow Collective]]*

The Shadow Collective is Maul's whole organisation and his own name for it. It takes in the criminal syndicates, meaning Black Sun, the trading arm that works under the name Crimson Dawn and formerly the Pyke Syndicate, now its cast-offs known as Shadow Pykes. It also takes in the Mandalorian Death Watch under Gar Saxon, salvaged droid formations, clone legions saved from ageing by black market procedures, hired ground troops, and the Nightborn. The Nightborn are only one part of it: the order of Force-users based on Dathomir. They are not the Shadow Collective and they do not run it, though they are its most feared element and the part the Republic talks about most. The difference matters, because the Republic is at war with the Shadow Collective. It has never been at war with the Nightborn and has never outlawed them by name. In practice the distinction has mattered less than it sounds. Nearly every Nightborn the Republic has arrested was taken during a Collective operation. They are charged as criminals or as terrorists, like anyone else on the raid. The exceptions are few and they are the ones that matter. A small number were arrested for what they were and not for anything they had done. Those are the cases Senator Vess keeps track of.

### The discarded

*Main article: [[The Shadow Collective]]*

The Shadow Collective's strength is not exotic. Its strength is that it hires the people the Republic was finished with. The law no longer puts food on the table or gives your children a roof to sleep under. The Collective does.

Between 16 and 7 BBY the Republic turned more than five million clones out into the galaxy. They were new citizens on paper and more than half were elderly in the body, trained for a job that no longer existed, living on a pension worked out for a man in his thirties. The Senate debated protecting them four sessions in a row and passed nothing. Crimson Dawn offered contracts, quarters, rank, and medical care suited to men ageing at twice the ordinary rate. By the Republic's own cautious estimate, around ten percent took the deal and by 2 ABY around eighty-six thousand of them still work for the Collective.

A large number of Shadow Collective ground units are commanded by men the Republic grew, freed, and then could not find a use for. Many Collective soldiers are rigorously trained by those same men, and getting deadlier by the year. The Collective also sponsors Ko Sai's procedure for all clones working for it. It is genome work followed by months of drug therapy to make the changes hold. It's done once, and after that every man takes a maintenance course of pills priced so low that nobody notices it. Naturally, the price is not the issue, but the availability is. Even if a clone purchases the procedure with independent funds, he will need to keep returning to the Collective for refills. For an old man, the procedure halves their accelerated ageing. If done when the clone is young enough, it returns him to ordinary ageing altogether.

The Concord of Raxus required the droid armies to be scrapped and the job was never finished. The Shadow Collective finds that hardware, repairs it and fields it. It is twenty years out of date, it is almost free, and there is a very great deal of it. Since the droid papers came out in 6 BBY there has also been an argument attached to it. The Republic scrapped them, despite the fact that some of them were self-aware beings. The Collective repairs them, gives them orders, and supposedly even gives some units rank. The practice might be performative, but does wonders for public perception.

The rest of the recruiting pool is simpler. Rim worlds that the Concord promised self-rule and the Senate then forgot to pay for. Separatist officers pardoned straight into unemployment. Mandalorians who have lost three civil wars. And the ordinary poor of countless systems, who are offered solid wages for work that carries a real chance of dying, which is more than the Republic offers them at all.

### The Shadow Troopers

*Main article: [[The Shadow Troopers]]*

The Republic kept a small number of clone units for covert and illegal operations. They were called shadow troopers, equipped with the best weapons and experimental black-and-red armours. They were sent against targets that the Judicial Forces or Clone Army could not be seen near: Republic politicians, inconvenient planetary lords and allied infrastructure that needed to be blown up so that its owner would bend to the demands of the Senate. They were used often and without care for casualties, because a man with no next of kin generates no questions and might as well have never existed. By the end of the Long War, the shadow troopers knew more about how the Republic actually worked than most senators did.

And for all their work and discretion, they were discharged just like everybody else in 16 BBY. Those that were not terminated for knowing too much, that is. Understandably, they were among the first clones to go to Maul, long before Wolffe began recruiting, and they decided to keep the name and the armours. The joke was that they would be a walking testament and mockery of the Republic's corruption. They would go on doing the same work the Republic commanded them to, just this time out in the open, for people who actually hired and compensated them.

The original companies were small and they were old before the Shadow War began. By 2 ABY very few of them are still alive. They either retired, became drill instructors or are coordinating Collective operations in purely tactical capacity. Over the years, most clones that joined the Collective took on a tradition to paint over their old legion's colours with the black and red of the shadow troopers. It's a gesture of both solidarity under a new employer and a way to honour those who were the first to throw a middle finger into the Republic's face. Even if most clones that do this have never met a real shadow trooper.

### Why it is winning the argument

*Main article: [[The Shadow Collective]]*

The Republic is a system of rules that has spent twenty years learning to tie its own hands. Anything it attempts to do has to survive an investigating office, a ninety-day vote and four committees. Its charter works. That is the trouble. It works at the speed it was built to work at, and it was built by people whose worst fear was a government that could move too fast.

The Shadow Collective has no charter. It takes in whatever the Republic throws out. It changes its roster, bosses and bases week to week. It holds no ground it cannot walk away from and has never once had to explain itself to anybody. In a galaxy of law, orders and structures, that would be a weakness. In this one, chaos has been their strength for many years.

### The Hutts

*Main article: [[The Hutt Clans]]*

The Hutt Clans deal with the Shadow Collective constantly and have never once submitted to it. Jabba Desilijic Tiure has done business with Crimson Dawn for eighteen years. He defers to Maul in nothing and treats the whole relationship as trade between equals, which infuriates Maul and is probably the point.

Open fights are frequent and settle nothing: contested spice routes, the Nal Hutta dock seizures, and the four-year dispute over who owned the Pykes, which the Pykes ended by selling themselves to both sides at once. That arrangement lasted until 4 BBY. The Hutts survive because they are older, because they are satisfied with steady business, and because Maul was unable to prove that killing them would be more profitable than dealing with them. And on top of that, the galactic chaos makes the Republic completely unable to interfere within Hutt Space.

## VIII. Dathomir

### The return

*Main article: [[Dathomir]]*

Dathomir was massacred by General Grievous in 21 BBY. The Nightsister clans were finished as an organised power and the survivors scattered. Maul was a son of that world, taken from it as a child and raised as a weapon by a master who threw him away. He came back in 10 BBY and spent four years rebuilding. He cleared the ruins and found survivors across a dozen sectors. The most important of them was Merrin, who had been seven years old when the massacre happened and had spent eleven years as the last practising witch of her clan.

### The Nightborn

*Main article: [[The Nightborn]]*

The order's name was a statement of intent. The Nightsister tradition had been closed to men for four thousand years. The Nightbrothers of the Dathomiri villages had been breeding stock and labour for it. Maul himself had been born into that arrangement, one he would not be willing to fund, so Merrin opened the practice to them. She chose Nightborn because Nightsister no longer described what she was building, and the word spread outward from her branch to cover the whole order. Three traditions were braided together. The magick came from Merrin: illusion, spirit-work, ichor, and the raising of the dead. Sith techniques came from Maul and from what he dug up at over a dozen ancient ruins. Jedi training, meditation, the lightsaber forms and a working knowledge of how the Jedi actually operate were brought in by defectors and taught by Barriss Offee. Offee had bombed the Jedi Temple in 19 BBY and was tried for it. At her trial she argued that the Order had become an army and had stopped listening to the Force. The demilitarisation of 16 BBY proved her right, and her life sentence was successfully appealed to a decade. She was released in 9 BBY, and within a month, Maul managed to contact her and sway her to his side. Apparently they talked over some tea. Barriss has not carried a lightsaber since her imprisonment and refuses to be called a Sith or a Jedi. By every account given from people around her, she genuinely believes in the Nightborn values. She is the reason the order has discipline, method and a curriculum.

The Nightborn's founding principle was the rejection of the Rule of Two and dark side fanaticism of the old Sith order. No Nightborn would ever again be anyone's tool. Dathomir was remade as a place to realise your own potential, discover your personal purpose, and share what you have discovered with those you wish to teach. No ranks, no orders and no duty to take part in anything. Every collective action has to be argued for with whichever Nightborn are present and willing to listen. There is only one rule: a Nightborn who finds another in danger is obligated to help. If you break the only rule set in place, you forfeit being a Nightborn. And the order owes nothing to strangers.

By 2 ABY, there are roughly six hundred Nightborn, of whom around four hundred are former Jedi. This is one of the most feared groups in the galaxy and they would all fit inside a small transport cruiser. Importantly, Nightborn are not exclusively dark side users. Each of them wields the Force in whichever way suits them most, though in practice there are almost none that completely abstain from the dark side. As such, many use sorcery to infuse their kyber crystals with Dathomiri ichor, turning them into ichor crystals that emit green smoke and a sickly glow when ignited. They are better at channelling the dark side than regular crystals and much easier to produce than bleeding a crystal the Sith way, though some Nightborn still choose that path. Many of the Nightborn that came from the Jedi Order still use their old Jedi sabers, and some new recruits elect to use vibro-weapons enhanced with Dathomiri magick.

The Nightborn take in Force-sensitives much as the Jedi do, but are far less careful about who they take. Adults are accepted and so are children, the latter of which are cared for by Barriss personally. Maul's standing order to the Collective is to watch for the signs and to test for midi-chlorians. Potential prospects are sent on to Dathomir. The Collective recruits in the poorest systems in the galaxy, so for most of those families this offer is the best one they will ever receive. That is why, even with the lax recruitment rules of the Jedi Templars, the Nightborn are still the fastest growing Force-user order in the galaxy.

### The Nightsisters

*Main article: [[The Nightsisters]]*

Asajj Ventress was reported killed during a Jedi operation in 19 BBY. She reappeared in 9 BBY, revealed to have been operating as a bounty hunter under multiple aliases for all this time. The same year she received an invitation from Maul. The rationale was simple. She and Maul were the same thing, Dathomiri weapons made by one hierarchy and thrown away by another. She was a bounty hunter because she had nowhere else to be and nothing to strive for. Maul could give her both. She accepted. She was one of the order's founders and for six years its best field leader.

The arrangement failed for the reason such arrangements usually fail. Maul had built it on a promise that nobody in it would be used, and used her exactly as Dooku had. They fought at Nightspire in 3 BBY. Neither died. She left the same night with twenty Dathomiri witches of the fifty present. Two hundred others went with her, Collective personnel, civilians and a handful of former Jedi. She set herself up in the eastern highlands, where she took back the name Nightsisters and the whole of the old practice with it: the closed rite, the female line, the four-thousand-year tradition Merrin had broken. She holds that the Nightborn are not a restoration of Dathomir but a second theft of it. Not because of the inclusion of men in the rites, but because they don't realise that Maul will forsake all of their principles the moment he needs an army. Neither Maul nor the Republic has been able to shift her, and both have decided it is not worth what it would cost to try.

## IX. Voss

### The veil

*Main article: [[Voss]]*

The world is on no Republic chart and has not been for four thousand years. Its people are unusually strong in the Force. Their mystics have kept up a concealment over the entire system, without a break, since the Old Republic. It is not a jamming field and it is not a scrambled navigation chart. It is worked directly through the Force. It turns attention away from that stretch of space without leaving any sign that it has done so. Ships do not fail to find Voss; they fail to look for it.

The Voss did this after the Sith and the Jedi both came for them in the ancient wars and neither left anything behind worth keeping. They decided to be absent from galactic history, and they held to it for four thousand years. This deception would have failed, if there was still someone in the Galaxy who understood the Force to the same degree that the Voss did.

### The withdrawalists

*Main article: [[Order of Voss]]*

The six hundred and thirty-two Jedi who resigned after the Ossus Compact in 8 BBY had lost an argument they thought was the only one that mattered. The Order's ruin came from serving a government, from becoming soldiers instead of monks, and no amount of tinkering with doctrine could help while it went on doing so. Having lost it, they stopped arguing. They gave up their rank, their access, their sabers and they scattered into the galaxy.

Over the next two years most of them arrived, by separate roads, at the same practice. A stripped-down discipline with no institution behind it and no membership. They had no purpose beyond silencing themselves and, for once, paying attention to what the Force willed. What many of them reported separately, between 6 and 5 BBY, was a place in the Force where their attention would not go. A picture drawn not by paint, but by patches of blank canvas. Some went there. Roughly two hundred of them made the crossing.

### The Order of Voss

*Main article: [[Order of Voss]]*

The welcome was not warm. The Voss had spent four thousand years making sure this exact thing would not happen, and the arrivals were, whatever they called themselves now, Jedi. There was confinement, a long argument, and at least one death that neither side has ever discussed. What settled it was a vision. A Voss mystic had seen them coming some years earlier and had chosen not to mention it until they arrived. The Voss treat visions as instructions, but revealing them too early can cause undue steps and excessive worry. Mystics reveal their visions when they deem the time is right. The arrivals were let in on that basis and on no other.

What has grown out of it in the years since is the Order of Voss: perhaps six hundred members now, Voss and human and a dozen other species, holding no territory outside the veil and claiming none. They take no commissions and accept no government's authority. They are not an army in any sense. They go out in ones and twos as healers, peacemakers and protectors. They work wherever they find work. They do not say where they come from and do not go back anywhere that can be followed. Every single one of them has given up their lightsabers, but foolish are those that equate being unarmed with being defenceless. Two hundred are former Jedi. The rest are Voss, and a number of people the members brought back with them since. The order takes in adults who ask and who are then vetted by visions of the Mystics. It seeks out nobody. Almost half of the members have never held a lightsaber and would not know what to do with one.

Master Aayla Secura is among them and is understood to have some standing there. She has not been seen in Republic space since 8 BBY and has sent no word to anyone she left behind. They have turned up at four recorded incidents in Republic space since 3 BBY and at least two in Shadow Collective territory. The Voss have refused contact with both, consistently and without hostility. Republic Intelligence keeps a thin file. The Ossus Council knows they exist, has made three approaches, and has been politely turned down three times.

## X. The Battle of Yavin

*Main article: [[The Battle of Yavin]]*

### The dig

Maul registered a dig on the fourth moon of Yavin Prime with the Republic Bureau of Antiquities in 5 BBY. It went through a shell company, on the correct forms, with the correct fees, renewed every year. It was one of eleven hundred such permits active in the Outer Rim that year and drew no attention whatsoever. The Massassi temples there were raised by the Sith of Naga Sadow's day and abandoned for four thousand years. They are also where Exar Kun put his spirit at the end of the Great Sith War. He escaped the destruction of his body by pouring himself into the stone, and he stayed there for four thousand years. Maul had not gone to Yavin for riches or power. He had gone to find out how Kun did it.

Maul is fifty-six as of 2 ABY. He has named no successor and has turned the question aside for a decade. His own lieutenants read this as vanity or weakness. It is in fact both, in a sense. Naming a successor admits there will need to be one. What he found at Yavin is a method for immortality for a price he will not pay. What Kun did held him to a place. Four thousand years fixed to a temple he could not leave is not survival as Maul understands the word. It is an eternity of imprisonment.

He has spent seven years looking for a version of the rite that does not carry that price. However, he has still uncovered troves of Sith knowledge for the Nightborn's benefit. Nobody in the Shadow Collective knows Maul's end goal. Neither Merrin nor Barriss Offee knows it. Both have privately concluded that he is simply afraid to name an heir because it would mean acknowledging his own age, and neither of them is wrong about that. As Maul's case proves, the legacy of paranoia does seem to run from master to apprentice among the Sith.

### The decision

Yularen closed his file on Tarkin in 2 BBY. In the first weeks of 1 BBY he laid the Outland Command evidence in front of the Chancellor and the investigators: the manifests, the accounts, the pattern of deaths. Yularen has been working with rogue Jedi Knight Do'Kass for the past decade, the Force-user's unconventional methods producing evidence that would otherwise not see the light of day. Tarkin understood his position at once. He did not deny the connection. He described it as wartime intelligence work, difficult compromises he was forced to make, and expressed regret that it had been necessary. Within four months he had become the loudest and most convincing voice in the Republic for destroying the Shadow Collective. He was even willing to fully fund and coordinate the manhunt for Maul, as long as the Republic provided troops. He was the man who had seen the danger first, from the inside, and whose warnings had been ignored all this time.

It was an extraordinary performance for the investigators and it mostly worked. He was given command of the operation, and the whole of it would be recorded for Republic supervision. And no doubt, what they hoped would be future propaganda. His gracious offer of funding and logistics meant it did not need formal authorisation from the Senate at all. Their hands would be clean, or so they thought.

### The Jedi who went

The Council refused to be involved. The debate ran for four days and the refusal was not close. The Order had abolished its own military rank sixteen years earlier and had spent eight years arguing about what it was for. It was not going to answer a Republic call-up. Thirty-one Jedi went anyway, as individuals, without permission of the Council.

Obi-Wan Kenobi went because he had thirty years of Maul behind him. He understood better than anyone alive what Maul would become with recovered Sith knowledge in his hands. Mace Windu went for reasons he did not give the Council but which are not hard to work out. He had acted once without permission, in 19 BBY, and had been tried for two years and proved right. The Council disowned their action publicly on the day the fleet jumped. It did not expel them, but it has never taken the disavowal back.

### Six days

The force went in badly briefed. What Tarkin knew about Nightborn capabilities was extensive, but he could not pass it on without further incriminating himself. So the briefing described a fortified criminal stronghold held by an army of Mandalorians and mercenaries. It did not describe a Sith nexus or the witches. The relevant pages of the intelligence file were held back by Tarkin's people on the grounds of protecting a source. So working off of this, the Republic sent a force of eleven thousand four hundred elite troopers, the best of what they had available and what they could spare. The landing on Yavin 4 was unopposed. The temple complex was taken on the second day at minimal cost. On the third night the Sith nexus was used to amplify Dathomiri magick.

The sixteen hundred survivors give accounts that are fragmentary and do not agree with one another. The Jedi who were there have refused to describe it without consulting the Order first. What is established is that the ground force was destroyed over about nine hours, and that the dead did not stay down. Republic soldiers killed in the assault were raised and turned on the second wave, which had landed at dawn expecting to reinforce a victory. Twenty-six of the thirty-one Jedi were killed. All twenty-six were raised. Mace Windu was supposedly among them.

What the ordinary soldiers experienced on that moon is covered in Section XIII, along with the reason for six hundred of the survivors to come out of this alive. Obi-Wan Kenobi held the northern landing zone for nineteen hours and gave the orders that had to be given there. He was fifty-seven at the time.

Anakin Skywalker, disgraced sixteen years ago, had arrived a day ahead of the fleet, uninvited and unannounced, and spent the last three days pulling people out of the collapse. On the sixth day Tarkin ordered the whole temple valley shelled from orbit. He did not pull anyone out first. More than a thousand living Republic soldiers were still inside the target area. The order could be defended in military terms and was. Most Nightborn left the moon hours earlier. Maul was already gone by day four.

### Aftermath

Nine thousand eight hundred elite Republic troops were dead, of whom over a thousand were killed by Republic guns. Twenty-six Jedi died. Yavin 4 was never retaken and is still surrounded by an interdiction fleet. The tragedy was devastating because it was recorded and the footage was all over HoloNet within hours. For the past nineteen years, the Republic tried to build an image of itself in which a great danger had been faced, defeated and legislated against. Yavin ended that narrative in six days, which the Republic will never recover from. The calendar was reset within the year, first by the news services and finally by law. There was no longer any useful way to describe the period that had just closed except as the time before this tragedy.

## XI. The Secessions

### Seswenna, 1 ABY

*Main article: [[The Secessions]]*

The bad briefing was established in the last months of 0 ABY. The investigators worked from the log of what had been withheld rather than from the missing pages themselves. The Outland Command's secret arrangement with Maul was published alongside it. Tarkin was summoned to answer for both, but Seswenna did not hand him over.

The sector had been run by Tarkin for eleven years. It was prosperous, orderly and safe. It had cleaned up its own droid remnants without Republic help. It had successfully pushed back against Collective and Hutt incursions. The people of Seswenna saw the Core's investigation as an attempt to tarnish the name of the man who had given them almost twenty years of stability. The Republic lost almost ten thousand soldiers due to its own incompetence and needed someone to blame. Most of the sector believed this sincerely, and all news that reached Eriadu through legal channels reinforced their stance. Seswenna's government voted to leave the Republic in the fourth month of 1 ABY. Seven neighbouring sectors supported it and voted for their own secessions as well, soon after forming a sector confederation, the independent Greater Seswenna Union.

There was no clause in the charter for the Republic to point at and none to break, so there was also no real way to stop it. Tarkin had spent twenty years building the largest concentration of military strength outside the Core. The Republic was fighting a new kind of war, and mounting a retaliatory force against Seswenna would only make things worse. The Senate condemned the withdrawal, refused to recognise it, and did nothing else. Almost all of the Core still trades with the Union, as the Eriadu hyperspace crossroads is too vital to simply abandon. The trade runs mostly one way. The Union buys more shipments from the Inner Rim every quarter, much of it rare minerals. Everyone with adequate political acumen knows why the Union works, and it is not because it's an autocratic military state (though many wannabe tyrants claim it is). It succeeds because it is younger than the Republic, its structures are appropriate for its smaller size, and it has focused on internal subjugation instead of outward expansion. So far.

Tarkin is now sixty-six. He holds the title of Governor-General of the Greater Seswenna Union and has never been convicted of anything by any court. He has given two long interviews about Yavin. In both of them he expressed deep regret but accepted no responsibility. In the realm of galactic politics, everyone noticed the Seswenna precedent. By the end of 1 ABY, at least seventy-eight sectors had formally asked their own lawyers and Senate representatives whether they could do the same. None has gone further than asking at that time. Except two.

### Quelii and Gordian Reach, 2 ABY

*Main article: [[The Secessions]]*

Senator Ilai Vess of the Quelii sector, which contains Dathomir, stood up in the Senate in the second month of 2 ABY and read the Bureau of Antiquities permit for the Yavin 4 dig into the record. Her case ran as follows. The site had been legally established for five years, registered, renewed and fully in order. No war had been declared on it. Nobody had obtained a warrant. Nobody had been given notice. A Republic fleet under Republic officers had attacked a licensed dig in a sector close to her own with no legal authority to do it. The people at the site had defended themselves with what they had, which she admitted was unusual, but which she would not condemn. The people of Yavin 4 were victims of unprovoked aggression and were within their right to repel the aggressors by whatever means necessary.

She then read more into the record. The Murkhana findings. The Outland Command arrangement. The deaths of Senator Fang Zar and the Ord Mantell magistrate. The droid papers of 6 BBY, which she read out in full, and which took most of an afternoon. And the names of the thirty-one rogue Jedi. The Republic's answer was that the Jedi at Yavin had been private individuals acting alone and that the Order had disowned them within hours. Vess replied that the Republic had made their surviving commander a general eleven weeks later, and that it had since put twenty-one hundred more Jedi into uniform and handed them the Coruscant Temple. She said that a government which promotes the officers of an operation it calls unauthorised, and then raises an army out of their colleagues, has already told the Senate what it really thinks of that operation.

Quelii voted to leave the Republic the same month. The declaration named several Shadow Collective assets as mercenary armies in the pay of the new state. Additionally, on behalf of the Gordian Reach sector as its contracted legal representative, Vess demanded the interdiction fleet be withdrawn immediately and asked for damages in a sum widely described as theatrical. The sum has not come down since. A month later the Gordian Reach itself seceded as well, quietly and without fanfare. The two sectors formed a defensive and economic alliance, the Dathomir-Yavin Accord, in the sixth month of 2 ABY. Three months later, the Thesme, D'Astan and Kalamith sectors (which lie between Quelii and the Gordian Reach) stopped remitting taxes towards the Republic. None of the three has been given a seat in the Senate for decades. Rumours circulate of increased Collective presence in those sectors and an expanded alliance pact being drafted by Oleth Kar, senator of the Gordian Reach. Everyone knows what this means, but the Republic won't acknowledge it, because it would mean the Republic just lost control of another vital hyperspace lane.

The Republic has not found an effective reply. It cannot prove a registered permit was not registered or that the attack was authorised when no order authorising it exists. It cannot claim that Murkhana and Ord Mantell did not happen. It cannot object to the use of the Force in war while employing Obi-Wan Kenobi as a general, and that is before anyone mentions the extensive use of Jedi during the Clone Wars. The Republic can say that Quelii's government is exploited by a criminal organisation. This seems entirely true yet impossible to prove, and forty years of Republic negligence in the Outer Rim would only make that argument a statement of the Republic's own incompetence.

By the end of 2 ABY the Ministry of the Interior had over sixty formal secession petitions sitting in review, nearly all of them from Outer Rim sectors. Over a dozen of them either directly border the Greater Seswenna Union or are in close proximity to it, most notably the Javin sector. The rest are isolated. Most are from systems that could not hold their own space for a week, not without the Collective's help at least. Each of them is being reviewed, because refusing to review them would itself be a decision. The Chommell sector, containing Naboo, filed one of those petitions, and is the only Mid Rim sector to do so.

Vess does not work for the Shadow Collective. She was born a Dathomirian, but was raised on Coruscant and never joined the Nightborn in any capacity. She is a legitimately elected senator, and she was the Gordian Reach's counsel in a case that sector was entitled to bring. What she did with the facts afterwards was her own decision. She has made a judgement about who can actually protect the people who elected her, and she has never once faltered in that stance.

### The standoff

*Main article: [[The Secessions]]*

The Republic will not recognise any of it. The refusal cost nothing in 1 ABY and now has become the most expensive decision the Republic has made. Because Seswenna never left, in law, its eight sectors are still entitled to eight Senate seats, though they have sent nobody since 1 ABY. Tarkin does not need the room anymore, though it is a weapon that is loaded for him to use. Quelii and the Gordian Reach are in the same situation and have taken the opposite view. Vess and Kar sit, speak, table motions and vote. All of it on the strength of a technicality they helped create and describe in public as an absurdity. Neither sector has remitted a credit of tax since it declared secession.

The Republic cannot bill them. Demanding levies from either sector means asserting that they are members in good standing, and a member in good standing is owed an answer on the reparations. Withdrawing the interdiction fleet from Yavin 4 would be read everywhere as recognition, and recognition concedes that the fleet had been sitting in foreign territory for two years. Leaving it there costs nothing and admits nothing, so it stays. Oleth Kar's invitation to remove it is not just a taunt. It is an offer he knows cannot be accepted.

The declaration is the part that cannot be conceded. Seswenna, Quelii and the Gordian Reach did not petition. They announced. Sixty sectors are currently asking permission, and the day the Republic recognises a sector that never asked, asking stops being necessary. Every course open to the Senate arrives at the same place at a different speed. It has chosen the one that arrives slowest.

### The Skywalker position

*Main article: [[Leia Skywalker]]*

Leia Skywalker took the first Chommell seat three months before Yavin, at eighteen, in 1 BBY. By 2 ABY she is the most quoted junior senator in the chamber and one of the most disliked. She has supported all three secessions in public and by name, and has voted against every motion condemning them. Her position is that a sector which can feed itself, police itself and defend itself has earned much greater autonomy, and that the Republic's insistence on supervising such sectors is not care but greed.

Her argument is not that the Republic is corrupt. She has read the same reports as everyone else and she thinks the charter works. That is the whole problem, as she puts it. The rules function exactly as designed, but the thing they are attached to is simply too large and hungry. Every year the machine gets slower, and every year more of the Rim decides it can do better alone. Both of those things are true at once, and neither is anybody's direct fault, which is why nobody will fix it. The goal should always have been to grow sectors into realms that could stand on their own, she says. Instead the Republic built a system that supervises forever and calls it care.

She supports the Gordian Reach reparations in principle, though she has also called the sum theatrical. Her position on the reparations is simple. If the money had gone to the Outer Rim when the Outer Rim needed it, there would have been nothing there for the Collective to build upon. The Outer Rim is becoming self-sufficient and rebellious because the Collective is providing absent services at a fraction of the cost the Republic would demand for it. Paying the reparation, even its vastly exaggerated sum, is the cheapest thing the Republic has been asked to do in twenty years.

She does not defend the Shadow Collective and has never been careless about the distinction. She calls them criminals in every speech, usually twice. The Nightborn are harder. She has said publicly that an order which has never been outlawed is not at war with anybody, and that the Republic should say so out loud before it stops being true. She has received three private invitations from Barriss Offee, the first in 1 ABY. She has declined all three and reported all three to the SIS, which is the only reason anyone knows about them. Padmé Amidala wrote the charter that Leia rages against. Her daughter spends most of her working life arguing that it is strangling the thing it was built to protect.

### Three stances of the Senate

*Main article: [[Three Stances of the Senate]]*

The Senate has settled into three political positions since Yavin and the secessions. None of them is a party in the old sense and all three cut across sector lines.

The Traditionalists hold that the Republic works. Sevrin Dal is one of them. The charter is sound and the Convention fixed what needed fixing. The only reason anything fails now is that sectors keep walking out. Bring them back and the problem ends. They are right that the charter works as written and right that the secessions broke the budget. They speak of the Rim as territory mislaid rather than as members who left. What they do not say is that bringing sectors back by force means a full civil war, and it means ruling by fear and threat.

The Republic Reformists hold that the idea of the Republic is sound but its machinery is not. Centuries of law have compounded into something nobody can read, and it fails more the closer to the edge of the galaxy you are. Their answer is to rebuild the system from the ground up, so that distance from the Core stops deciding how fairly a world is treated. Sectors would then want to stay. They have real results behind them, and several hundred archaic rules have been replaced with ones people can follow. In private they admit what finishing this process would cost. A generation of work, and the money that the Republic is spending on the war. They have never said what all the sectors that want to leave are supposed to do in the meantime.

The Core Isolationists agree that the system is broken and add that it is too small for what it carries. Their answer is full withdrawal of the Republic to the Core and the Inner Rim, focus shift towards self-sufficiency of the Core, and free trade with everything beyond it. They want the Mid and Outer Rim to gain full autonomy. They point at Seswenna, which left, still trades with the Core, and is doing better than ever. They point at the Kashyyyk Region, which rebuilt itself on just a tax cut. The Rim self-rule bloc votes with them constantly. Their case is the most honest about capacity and the least honest about consequence. None of them says aloud what everyone understands. Free trade between the technologically superior Core and the underdeveloped Rim is not equalising. It will result in flagrant abuse, just without laws to protect from it.

The three pull in three directions and nothing moves. Every emergency renewal passes because the traditionalists want the war and the other two cannot agree on an alternative. Leia Skywalker votes with the reformists on every law. She votes with the isolationists on every question of who should govern the Rim. She has never joined either.

### How the Collective thrives

*Main article: [[The Shadow Collective]]*

The Collective has absorbed several hundred local syndicates and crime families since 12 BBY and has rarely turned one away. They remain what they were, criminals through and through. Every one of them runs protection, moves contraband and kills people who become inconvenient. What changed is the standard they are measured against. A syndicate that keeps a hospital open and a port running has outdone forty years of Republic rule.

There is no law underneath, only temperament. Some bosses behave like landlords who intend to keep their property nice and orderly. They repair what they use and pay for schools their own people attend. They still take far more out than they put back in, but that is more than the Republic ever gave. Others are warlords and rule accordingly, with a whip and an iron fist. A world's fortune depends entirely on which of them it fell to.

Maul does not regulate this and does not care to. Every one of them is a criminal, and the only thing that can be asked of a criminal is if the money is right. And it always is. Nadi Orl audits all of them quarterly and checks if the ledgers are in order. If anything is amiss, they are warned once and severely fined. If they fail again, they are eliminated and replaced.

## XII. The Shadow War

### What kind of war it is

*Main article: [[The Shadow War]]*

The Senate never declared war and could not have. In 0 ABY there was no state to declare it against. The Shadow War is the only name anyone has for it. The name came from a news broadcast in the first weeks and moved into the Senate record within the year. By 2 ABY the Ministry uses it in its own paperwork. It is not a war of fronts, but there are battles, of course. Fifty-two engagements in two years would be recognised as such by Republic records. But that is not where the real conflict is at. It is not like the Clone Wars, where millions of bodies were crushed under billions of droids. The scale is smaller, and the methods are different.

The Shadow Collective has no military targets to lose because it has no military assets to defend. No vital worlds. No fleet worth engaging. No depot that matters. What it has instead is presence. Every settled world is a source of money and recruits. A dock that skims cargo. A magistrate on retainer. A quartermaster selling parts out of a garrison store. None of that can be bombed. They latch onto independent infrastructure, embed themselves into it and then benefit from it. They want the Rim booming with commotion, industry and trade, and have even invested their own funds to facilitate it, with the bulk of the credits going into charting new hyperspace routes. They stopped thinking like criminals and started to think like an empire, and it's working.

So the fighting is done by auditors, customs inspectors and sector rangers, with the occasional raid. It is done everywhere at once and it never stops. A citizen of the Core can go a lifetime without seeing a battle and will pay Collective money every week without knowing it, in the price of shipping and the cost of a permit. This is why the war is unpopular in a way the Clone Wars never were. There is no line on a map that is moving. There is only the discovery, every few months, that something you relied on had already been seized.

The galaxy is very large and almost entirely empty. Perhaps one star system in a hundred has a world anyone can live on, and most inhabited worlds hold a single town, a mine or a few farms. The census of 4 BBY counted about eighty billion people in the Republic, with everyone else in the charted galaxy coming to perhaps two billion more. On ninety-five percent of worlds, nothing has ever happened that necessitated a soldier's intervention. The Shadow War is fought over that meaningful five percent. When measured against the whole galaxy, every number in the war looks small, but when measured against the places that matter, it starts to make sense.

### Why Quelii cannot be attacked

*Main article: [[The Shadow War]]*

Quelii is the one place that looks like a target. Dathomir is the closest thing the Collective has to a capital and everything the Nightborn are is made there. Taking it would be expensive but it would not be impossible. If the Republic committed itself, it could probably do it. That is not what stops it. The Republic's strength was never its army or fleet. It was that a thousand sectors believed membership was safer than leaving. Nothing enforces that belief. There is no garrison in most of the Rim and there never has been. Order holds because the Republic has spent centuries being the benevolent authority that does not come for you.

The day it burns a sector that left peacefully, that stops being true everywhere at once. And the sectors watching will learn that asking is dangerous, let alone leaving. They will stop filing petitions and start quietly withholding levies and taxes, because that is what a sector does when it has decided the Core is unreliable. Some are already doing that. Every credit withheld leaves the Republic less able to answer the next threat. The weaker it looks, the more sectors conclude, correctly, that it will not come for them either. It ends in slow dissolution or in a war against its own members. Nobody in the Ministry has produced a model where it ends anywhere else. So the fleet sits at Yavin 4 and Quelii is not attacked.

The Collective understands this as clearly as the Republic does. Inside Quelii's borders its conduct has been immaculate. No extortion, no seizures, no unexplained deaths. Fees paid, contracts honoured, disputes taken to Quelii courts. All of the Collective's troops in Quelii are employed under legally established mercenary companies, with pensions and veteran funds. Republic Intelligence has spent two years looking for one clean incident of Collective aggression against the Republic inside Quelii and has not produced one. Outside those borders the syndicate has done all of it, constantly, rampaging through thousands of systems.

### The Republic at war

*Main article: [[The Shadow War]]*

Chancellor Sevrin Dal asked for emergency powers eight days after the last survivors came home from Yavin. The Senate granted them and has renewed them eight times. He is blatantly pushing for war against Quelii, but has not managed to sway the Senate just yet.

The ninety-day rule has worked exactly as written on every single occasion. Padmé Amidala, who wrote it, has said that the rule works and the politics beat it. She has not proposed changing it, on the grounds that she cannot think of a change that would have made any difference.

### The Republic Corps

*Main article: [[The Republic Corps]]*

The Senate began raising the Republic Corps in 18 BBY, within weeks of the Kamino findings. The work took two years. Cancelling the clone contract in 17 BBY was one step of it.

The Corps was not ready in time for most of the Long War, so the war was finished by the Auxiliary Corps. Its regiments were filled with clones who had signed on again and conscripted citizens given only a few weeks of training. They are the ones that took most of the war's last casualties. They were disbanded between 15 and 12 BBY, though there was not much left to disband.

The Republic Corps was built in the middle of that war, so it was created out of what already existed and was readily available. And that something was the Senate Commandos, a small elite unit of the Senate Guard. They had never been part of the clone army; all of them were recruited volunteers and none of them had the public perception baggage that came with being a clone. The Republic took their armour blueprints, their training regimens and their colours, and simply upscaled all of it into a standing army. The ceremonial Senate Guard was left as it was. When the Commandos left the Senate building, the Guard took over all security responsibilities.

The familiar armour was the point, as the public already trusted it. A recruit in 16 BBY wore not just armour, but a cultural statement. Republic citizens who saw them simply assumed they were elite troops. Every enlisted soldier holds the rank of commando, and the title is not only for show. A clone had to be only good enough to justify the batch he came from. The Corps could choose the best of the best from a large and varied pool of volunteers. They did and they were not afraid to put the ones they chose through the wringer. About six in ten finish the training, and the rest are sent home. On average a commando is better at the job than the clone he replaced. Whatever else the Republic economised on, it did not spare any expense on the Republic Corps.

Ranks are marked as increasingly elaborate white markings on the armour. The fancier the design, the more important a commando is. Marshals adopt some elements from the Senate Guard, adding a cape over the left arm, a large crest on top of their helmets and a short ceremonial veil attached magnetically to the bottom of the helmet. The blue has never changed for any rank. Enlisting is called 'going blue'. The soldiers are 'the blues', or the 'boys and girls in blue'.

Field command has four ranks: commando, sergeant, captain and marshal. A marshal commands the garrison of one sector. Above the marshals sit the generals, who answer to the civilian ministry and do not take the field, although that was not always the case. Anakin Skywalker commanded at Murkhana as a boots-on-the-ground general in 16 BBY. The Constitutional Convention of 11 BBY ended the practice for good. No general has led troops in person since, with Obi-Wan Kenobi as the sole exception, and his commission had to be written specially to allow it.

Any citizen of a member world may enlist at the age of maturity for their species. The first term is six years. Since Yavin every recruit is tested for Force sensitivity, and so was everyone already serving. Those who test positive are offered to the Templars and most are recruited, though some reject the offer and come back to the Corps. Most recruits come from the Mid Rim and the poorer worlds of the Core. The Outer Rim sends almost none, because working for the Collective just pays better there.

The Republic has eight million under arms, one citizen in every ten thousand. That is a third more than the clone army numbered in 19 BBY, at three times the cost. A clone cost his growth, his armour and his rations. A commando costs a wage, a veteran pension and an allowance for their family. If they die, they will need a funeral, and such a funeral will make their neighbours think about the cost of war. Spread across a thousand sectors, the Corps would come to eight thousand soldiers each, but that is not how they are distributed in reality. The Core is fully garrisoned, and around half of the Inner Rim is too, mostly along its borders. Beyond that, in the Mid and Outer Rim, the Corps only garrisons tactically vital worlds and chokepoints along important trade lanes. Most of the Corps simply stands guard. The largest postings face the borders of the Greater Seswenna Union and the Collective foothold in Quelii and Gordian Reach. The interdiction fleet at Yavin 4 is the only Republic force inside either sector, and it cannot leave. The garrisons that were inside Seswenna were absorbed by the Union. The Shadow War itself is fought mostly by the Judicial Forces and the Sector Rangers. The Corps is called in when a raid needs numbers or a gang war escalates into an actual planetary war.

### The Judicial Forces and the Sector Rangers

*Main article: [[The Judicial Forces and the Sector Rangers]]*

The Republic's real strength on the ground is not the Corps, but the Judicial Forces and the Sector Rangers. The Judicial Forces serve the courts: they investigate, arrest and are entitled to pass judgement in local matters. The Sector Rangers patrol everything that the Corps doesn't: ports, roads, outlying settlements and the lanes between worlds. Between them they number about two hundred million, which is twenty-five times what the Corps can muster.

The catch is that neither is an army. Both are generously funded from Coruscant, but run from the ground. Most of their officers were local police and militias before 1 ABY. The Republic didn't recruit them per se. All it needed to do was pay them, arm them and deputise them by putting the Republic's name on their uniforms. Local authorities and each sector's Republic representatives decide who serves and where, so that Coruscant doesn't need to keep their names in an already bloated record. The Senate does not know how many of them there are on any given world, and they do not care.

That is also why they cannot be moved. A Ranger hired on Ord Mantell works on Ord Mantell, patrols the same streets for twenty years and goes home at the end of every shift. No Chancellor can send that person to Quelii. Only one part of either force answers to Coruscant directly. It is a small office for crimes that cross sector lines, led by chief criminal investigator Eogun Vidoc.

The Senate judges both organisations only by two numbers. One is how much money went into a sector, and the other is how fast its crime rate fell. Nothing else reaches Coruscant. Where the local authority is honest, the arrangement works very well. Where it is not, the Republic is unknowingly paying for the Collective's police, and the crime is still kept in check, just for a different reason.

### The Grand Union Army

*Main article: [[The Grand Union Army]]*

The Corps garrisons stationed in the eight sectors of the Greater Seswenna Union did not come home. There were about a hundred thousand commandos in them, which was much more than the sectors reasonably needed. Officers from Tarkin's old Outland Command had spent ten years arranging postings there, quietly accumulating military presence exactly where they wanted it to be. When the Union was declared, the soldiers were offered a universal one-step rank promotion and double the pay. Nobody could find a single person that rejected that offer. The Republic has never recognised the secession, so it has never listed those soldiers as deserters. In law they are still at their posts.

Naturally, a hundred thousand soldiers do not make an army, but they can make its officers. The sector forces Tarkin had raised as governor were folded in, and the rest was conscripted from the local population. Every able adult in the Union is liable to be called, if they do not already hold a job within the Union government or under Union-owned industry sectors. The training is much longer than the Corps' and far harsher, with a full-course success rate of only about ten percent. Those who fail are not punished, just sent home. But those that succeed are rewarded with so many privileges that failing might as well be resigning yourself to be a second-class citizen. The Union holds a little over seven hundred million people. By 2 ABY close to three million of them were under arms, with more in training. That is one citizen in every two hundred and fifty, while in the Republic it is one in ten thousand.

The Union Army is the state's only force on the ground. There are no Sector Rangers and no Judicial Forces under Tarkin's new government. The same elite soldiers who hold the sector borders are also the ones that patrol the streets of Eriadu. That is how the Union found the Collective's people inside it, and no other state has managed the same. The Union Army kept the Corps ranks from commando to marshal and added one above them, the First Marshal, which Deran Kolt holds.

The Union did not keep the blue uniforms of the Corps. Its soldiers wear white plates over a black bodyglove. The design began as a prototype third pattern of clone armour. It was in field trials with the Outland Command when the clone contract was cancelled in 17 BBY, and the local garrisons simply decided to keep it instead of sending it back to Republic armouries. Seswenna's engineers then spent ten years improving it in secret, and preparing the factories with proper blueprints. The new and improved Union Commando armour went into full production the same month that the Union was declared, in 1 ABY. Armour and weapons are identical throughout the army, with any kind of personalisation being against the army's strict policy. The only thing that tells one Union soldier from another is a small badge printed on both the chest plate and back-plate, which details a soldier's rank and identification number. Every single Union soldier is intensively trained to be able to read that identification in a split-second, even in minimal visibility conditions.

### The Jedi Templars

*Main article: [[The Jedi Templars]]*

Fifteen weeks after Yavin the Republic made the Order an offer: rank restored, the Coruscant Temple returned as a base of operations, and a formal place in the war. The Council at Ossus did not put it to a vote, since it was obvious within a day that a vote would break the Order in half whichever way it went. The Council ruled instead that every member would answer for themselves. The traditionalists were gone after eleven days, and roughly twenty-one hundred Jedi went back to Coruscant. About twenty-nine hundred stayed on Ossus.

The Coruscant body took the name Jedi Templars within the year. It began as an insult from Ossus. Once it was clear the insult was not going away, they took it as their own. Their doctrine has moved a long way in two years and is still moving. Rank is formal again and worn. Training has been reweighted towards combat, with the older contemplative programme cut back to bare necessities. A new addition to the curriculum, mandatory training in criminology, interrogation techniques and Force-forensics, has produced Jedi that are expert at combating the Collective.

The Ossus Compact has been set aside entirely. The Templars hold that loosening the attachment rules produced defections that reinforced the Nightborn, and they harshly enforce the old ways of discipline. However, they have begun taking adult recruits, mostly from Republic service families, which the Order has not done in eight hundred years. The Templar recruiters are constantly scouring the galaxy for Jedi that left the Order in the decade of 18 to 8 BBY, trying to convince them to protect the Republic once again.

They also moved back into a Temple with a Sith shrine under it. The shrine was sealed and reconsecrated in the first month and the Templars consider the matter closed. Ossus raises it in every communication between the two bodies without fail, and has been asked to stop pestering.

The Compact stood for eight years and people used it. The Jedi at Ossus married. Others found the families they had been taken from. Many of them are still living with them. The Templars who came out of that generation either made no such ties or left them behind to go to Coruscant. It is not unheard of for a Templar to eventually have to face down their own resentful family, now wearing Nightborn robes. To the Templars, such tragedies only reinforce their stance on attachments being a slow-acting poison.

Luminara Unduli leads them, taking on the old title of Grand Master. She has never pretended her reasons are purely institutional. She trained Barriss Offee, she did not see what was forming in her student, and she has said publicly that she should have. Every Force-sensitive the Nightborn have taken in was taught by Barriss from what Unduli taught her first. A journalist asked her in 1 ABY whether the Templars existed to fight the Shadow Collective or to fight her former student. She said both, and that she could see no useful distinction. Ki-Adi-Mundi and Stass Allie went with her and currently sit on her council. Both had been generals in the Clone Wars and neither had ever been reconciled to giving it up.

### Ossus

*Main article: [[Ossus Order]]*

Plo Koon presides over the Ossus Order, insofar as anyone does. He was the reformists' most persuasive voice through the schism years and the man who did most to get acknowledged bonds into the Compact. His grounds were straightforward. He had spent a war caring about the people under his command, took severe risks to save them, and had never once found that it made him 'corrupted' or worse at the job. He declined the Templar offer to join in a written reply of two sentences, which has been widely circulated since: "The path you are determined to re-tread will destroy you. We will not follow."

Shaak Ti stayed with him. She went to Kamino in 18 BBY and read the specifications for what the Republic's army had been built out of. Her position ever since is that the Order will not be putting itself back into Republic hands in her lifetime. She was afraid to speak out during the Clone Wars. She failed Fives, and has vowed to never fail anyone like that ever again. Coleman Kcaj also stayed, and teaches. Aayla Secura has long since departed to Voss before any of this happened and knows nothing about it.

Ossus is not absent from the Shadow War and does not claim to be, but it does claim neutrality. It treats the wounded from both sides. It has refused four Republic requests for intelligence and has quietly evacuated three Rim settlements ahead of Collective sweeps. What it will not do is take a commission.

Perhaps a hundred children with the potential to become Jedi are born in the Republic each year. Where a Force-sensitive child is found now decides what becomes of them. Near the Core the Templars will hear of it first. In the Rim the Collective usually hears first, and the child goes to Dathomir, though the Ossus Order tries to find them first. The Voss only accept people who seek them out through the Force.

### Kenobi

*Main article: [[Obi-Wan Kenobi]]*

Obi-Wan Kenobi was made a general of the Republic in the eleventh week after Yavin, four weeks before the Templars existed. The Republic wanted him for four reasons and was open about three of them. He was the senior surviving officer of the operation, a Jedi Master, and the only man alive who had the authority to describe what had happened on the ground. He had spent ten years as the Order's public voice in the Senate hearings, which meant that every household in the Core knew his face and had been trained by a decade of holonet transmissions to believe him. And he knew Maul better than anyone alive, having fought him multiple times across thirty years.

The fourth reason went unstated. Jedi defectors that joined the Nightborn fought on Yavin 4 and the images had been broadcast. The Republic needed a Jedi standing on its side of the line, visibly and in uniform, and it needed one before the public settled into the view that the Jedi and the Nightborn were the same thing in different colours. Kenobi was the best candidate anybody could name.

He argued against the appointment for nine days. His objections were the ones everyone else would make later. The Order had refused the call-up, and commissioning one of the men who ignored that refusal would make the refusal meaningless. It would be quoted against the Republic by anyone looking for a reason to leave it. He was proved right about that within eighteen months. He accepted anyway, and his reason was narrower than the argument against it.

Thirty-one Jedi had gone to Yavin on a briefing that had been deliberately gutted. Twenty-six of them died because nobody in the chain of command knew what a Sith nexus was, and because the officers who did know were protecting Tarkin's reputation. If the Republic fought the rest of this war the same way, it would happen again, it would keep happening, and there was nobody else in any position to stop it.

He had held the northern landing zone for nineteen hours and given the orders that were given there. He has said once, and only once, that having done that he could not hand that burden to somebody else and go back to teaching. He understood the price and took it anyway. His appointment has been the strongest single piece of evidence in the Vess case since the day Vess stood up, and he knew it would be before he signed. He has asked to be released twice since.

He is not a Templar and has refused to become one three times. He is not at Ossus, which regards his commission as precisely the error it left Coruscant to avoid. He holds a Republic rank. He commands Republic soldiers and a few dozen Jedi who followed him rather than either faction. He belongs to nothing.

The other difficulty for everyone in Jedi uniform is that they are fighting people they trained. Collective field operations are coordinated out of proportion by Nightborn, who know Republic doctrine, Jedi tactics, and how to stay hidden from people they often studied beside. Master Quinlan Vos was embedded inside the organisation in 1 ABY by the Templars, who did not inform Ossus. He has sent information out at irregular intervals ever since. He has sent more information to Kenobi privately. Nobody has reached a settled view if Vos is still their asset. His well-known propensity to disregard Jedi creed is troublesome, but it has given credence to his alibi as a defector. His problematic attachment to Asajj Ventress, and over a dozen unsuccessful attempts to contact her, make his real loyalty and priorities an open question.

### Mandalore

*Main article: [[Mandalore]]*

Twenty-one hundred trained Force-users went into the field for the Republic in 0 ABY. The Nightborn number around six hundred. They are on average more skilled individually and they choose where and when to fight. And they will still lose a war of attrition by a wide margin. Everyone involved can do the arithmetic. The Shadow Collective's answer was not to make more Nightborn, but to stop needing them.

A lightsaber will not go through beskar. A solid slug does not stop for a light-blade the way a blaster bolt does. Both facts are a thousand years old and both have been largely forgotten. The great armouries were sealed in the southern ruins of Mandalore, and the Mandalorians who might have opened them had spent forty years losing wars to one another. Gar Saxon's Death Watch fought over the southern half of Mandalore, which is the half that contains the most ruins, and had been working the old vaults without much spare time since 4 BBY.

Bo-Katan Kryze fully took the north in 1 ABY. It was a real choice, made with her eyes open, and she has never doubted her reasoning. The north holds three quarters of Mandalore's people and most of the industrial infrastructure. In every respect except one, that was the right decision. However, with nobody left to contest the south, Death Watch spent the next year doing what they didn't have the leisure to do in a generation. It reopened the armouries. It refitted an army in beskar. It went back to re-learn techniques for killing Force-users that had survived only because a handful of isolationist sects had never stopped practising them.

One of those sects is the Children of the Watch, who take in orphans and raise them in the creed from infancy. They have done this without a break since before the Clone Wars. Until about 0 ABY most Mandalorians regarded them as a mild embarrassment. They are now the most valuable military asset the Shadow Collective possesses.

Din Djarin was orphaned in the Long War and taken in by the Children as an infant. He is twenty-one. He has been trained since he could walk to kill people who can predict the future and bend minds, and he is extremely good at it. The Templars confirm eight Jedi dead by his hand and privately suspect a good many more. He was born the same year as Luke Skywalker. They have not met yet.

### Tano

*Main article: [[Ahsoka Tano]]*

Ahsoka Tano had lived quietly in the Outer Rim under a name she did not advertise since 8 BBY, tired of the uselessness of Low Republic politics. Rex lived with her for three years. As he neared his biological fifties, his health began to decline from many badly treated wartime wounds. Ahsoka took care of him, until he passed away in his sleep in 5 BBY. In the sixth month of 0 ABY she left the place she had been living and has not been found since. What is known is thin and none of it is confirmed.

She is following Skywalker and she is behind him. She was on Vandor four weeks after he was and on Ryloth nine days after he was. Both were confirmed by people who recognised her and had no reason to invent it. She has not caught him. Nobody catches him. She is trying to intercept the one man in the galaxy who does not decide where he is going. She has also been to the edge of Dathomiri space at least twice. A ship matching no registry breached the Nightsister territory and stayed there for five hours in the ninth month of 1 ABY and then left. Nobody came out to see it. Ventress's people have said nothing about the matter.

There is no evidence that she has actually spoken to Ventress, to Merrin or to Barriss Offee. There is no evidence that she has reached Skywalker either. Nobody in the Republic knows what she is attempting. Investigator Vidoc has her filed as someone he would like to talk to, but doesn't yet have a reason to track her down.

### What each side fields

*Main article: [[The Shadow War]]*

What the Republic has grown since Yavin is not the Corps. The Judicial Forces were revived in 1 ABY after forty years of neglect and the Sector Rangers were properly funded for the first time in a century. The Ministry now spends the most on intel, private detectives, bounty hunters and security funds.

Its anti-Force programmes are the strangest line in the budget, fuelled by the paranoia of the Nightborn rising to prominence. Over a year and a great deal of money have gone into securing and cordoning off the planet Myrkr to gather ysalamiri, a species of lizards that push the Force out of a bubble around them, and roughly two hundred of those are currently alive off-world. Cortosis-weave gauntlets are standard issue to escort units that expect to run into Nightborn. They will short out a lightsaber for about eight seconds, which is long enough. Taozin nodules, which hide a wearer from Force detection, are prohibited to Republic personnel and are worn by perhaps a third of Republic Intelligence. Lastly, a lot of budget goes into finding and securing any Sith ruins, so that Nightborn don't get to them first.

The Collective's advantage is that almost everything it fields costs next to nothing. The Concord left something near four million droid hulls unaccounted for across thousands of systems. Around two hundred thousand have been recovered and returned to service. Three Lucrehulk hulls in the Gordian Reach have been stripped down for their foundries and communication hubs, bolstering the rate at which the Collective refurbishes droids. Cortosis-weave BXs were a Separatist experiment that never reached production and are now in production. Its best anti-Jedi asset is older than any of it: IG-100 MagnaGuards, built to Grievous's specification for killing Force-users, of which about nine hundred are operational. Then there are all the ancient Sith treasures that Maul dug up. Only a select few Nightborn know all the specifics, but they are known to be in possession of holocrons dating back to the ancient Sith Empire.

The difference in approach follows from the difference in cost. The Republic has to be everywhere and can only afford to pick targets. The Collective has no budget unless it actively extorts a population, so it fights only where it's already profitable to stay.

## XIII. Skywalker

### Murkhana, 16 BBY

*Main article: [[Anakin Skywalker]]*

As a general of the Republic Corps, not a Jedi or a Chosen One, Anakin destroyed a city. Intelligence said that there was a buried droid command node under it and that the population was complicit. The node was there. Whether the city's population knew about it is still debated to this day. About two hundred thousand civilians died and the campaign in that sector ended four months early. The intelligence had come to him through Outland Command. He said so at the inquiry. He said someone wanted him gone, but he could not prove it, and he sounded exactly like a man making excuses.

Skywalker's and Tarkin's war theatres overlapped for three years and that was enough to sign the former Jedi's sentence. Skywalker fought to finish the war and Tarkin's position depended on the war continuing. The most effective commander in the Republic was in the wrong theatre, moving too fast, closing campaigns that had been budgeted for years. Of course, nothing has ever been proved. Outland Command has no surviving record of sending the reports. The inquiry reached no conclusion. His wife's enemies wanted a conviction and her allies wanted it buried, and the compromise between them finished both of their public careers. He was discharged with honour in 14 BBY.

### What it did to him

*Main article: [[Anakin Skywalker]]*

The inquiry treated Murkhana as a question of fact and of who had authorised what. It never asked the other question, which is what the thing did to the man who did it. The accounts that matter were never entered into any record, because nobody was collecting them. They come from Padmé Amidala, from Kenobi before the estrangement, from Ahsoka, and from two officers of his staff. They agree in outline. He came out of Murkhana convinced that he was not safe to be near. Not that he had erred or that he had been set up, though he believed that too. He believed that he was, as a standing fact about himself, deadly to whatever he was closest to. After a two-year inconclusive investigation, he was discharged and he did not go home.

The separation from Padmé is usually described as a political arrangement, and it partly was. Her career could not survive standing next to him, but the decision was substantially his. The reason he gave was that a man who had killed two hundred thousand people on a briefing he wasn't wise enough to double check had no business raising two children. Padmé did not agree, and has never agreed. The record of that argument survives in C-3PO's testimony. The family was never estranged in the ordinary sense. They love him and he loves them, and he has decided that this is not the relevant consideration. He has visited the twins, but not often and never for long. By the end of 2 ABY he had not visited them for seven years.

### The prophecy

*Main article: [[Anakin Skywalker]]*

The other thing he did not recover from was the prophecy. He had been named the Chosen One and told he would bring balance to the Force. In 19 BBY the Sith were destroyed and he was not the one who destroyed them. He had stood in the room and failed to move. What that produced, over years, was not despair. It was something considerably worse. He concluded that the prophecy had not failed. It was still running. His part in it had not yet been handed to him. His task was therefore to stop deciding anything and let himself be moved.

He has done this for sixteen years. He does not plan. He does not accept commissions. He goes where he is pulled and does not explain, because he cannot. A Republic ranger on Vandor asked him in 4 BBY what he was doing there. He answered that he did not know, because it was not his choice to be there. The luxury of reason is reserved for those who choose.

### The record

*Main article: [[Anakin Skywalker]]*

He is clean, well-groomed and dressed in Jedi robes, but he is alone, and by every description given of him he is fundamentally unwell. He talks to people who are not present. Local authorities on eleven worlds have detained him and all eleven let him go. In most cases nobody could think what to charge him with. In three cases they were afraid of him. He is also entirely coherent, and this is the part that unsettles people who have met him. Everyone that knows him says he still has the same snark, humour and brashness. His reasoning is intact. His conclusions are frequently correct. He simply works from premises nobody else holds and will not argue about them. And then there is the record.

An analyst at the Office of the Inquiry compiled his confirmed appearances in 1 ABY. There were forty-one of them across fifteen years, and she set them against significant events. The paper was not published and the analyst was reassigned. Skywalker reached Ord Mantell eleven days before the Collective moved on the shipyards. He was in the building when the Coruscant procurement fraud broke in 6 BBY, and he was the reason the auditor got to a magistrate alive. He walked into the Cato Neimoidia hostage situation in 3 BBY with no indication that he knew there was one. He is as disruptive to the Republic as he is to the Collective, and the pattern shows no sign of caring about the difference. Four of the forty-one appearances ended the careers of Republic officials. All four of those officials were later shown to have been taking Collective money. Nobody has ever established what Anakin thought he was doing.

During the years of wandering he also wrote a twenty-page literary dissertation about the tale of Darth Plagueis the Wise, published under a pseudonym. The text has been well regarded in literary circles. The Ossus academy and the Jedi Templars both banned it from their curriculums. They knew who wrote it.

### The lightning

*Main article: [[Anakin Skywalker]]*

He has dreamed of Palpatine's death for twenty years. On the rare occasions that he described it, he said it was the same few minutes, repeating. Somewhere in that repetition he learned to do it. The first confirmed use of Force lightning by Skywalker was on Nar Shaddaa in 5 BBY, against Collective enforcers. He has done it many times since. The Jedi who have seen it do not dispute the identification. Nightborn have witnessed it since and also do not know what to make of it. Nobody can explain why it has done nothing to him.

Work of that kind leaves marks. It marked Palpatine, visibly, and the Sith before him. It marks the Nightborn, as they tap into the dark side, so readily available for them. Skywalker has been using the dark side freely for seven years, without technique and without restraint, and he is not corrupted by any measure anyone can apply to him. He is exactly as ruined as he was in 14 BBY and no further. The Templar position is that this is not possible and the reports must be mistaken. The Ossus position is that Skywalker is unprecedented and they will not cast judgement carelessly. An intermediary put the question to Barriss Offee in 1 ABY. She is reported to have called both of those answers comfortable. There is a third, she said. He may be entirely right about the prophecy, and that possibility should frighten people a great deal more than it does.

### Yavin

*Main article: [[The Battle of Yavin]]*

He arrived at Yavin 4 the day before the Republic fleet. Nobody sent him. Nobody knew he was coming. He had been seen four weeks earlier on Umbara and had given no indication of any destination. He fought at the temple complex on the second day. Most of the soldiers around him did not know who he was. Some officers did, and could do nothing about it. On the third night, when the ground force came apart, he was on the eastern approach.

### What the survivors describe

*Main article: [[The Battle of Yavin]]*

The Sith nexus did not confine itself to Force-users. This is the single most important thing in the Yavin record and the Republic has never known what to do with it. Eleven thousand four hundred ordinary soldiers were on that moon. Every survivor was tested afterwards, and none showed any measurable Force sensitivity, nothing that could be trained. From the third night onward almost all of them felt it. The choking terror of the dark side.

The descriptions converge on drowning. Weight on the chest. Cold in the mouth. A pressure that came from underneath and rotted their minds. Men who had never in their lives had an unusual experience reported the same one within an hour of each other, across forty kilometres of jungle. A hundred and ninety survivors have since been diagnosed with psychiatric conditions the Republic medical service has no scientific name for.

And in the middle of all the horrors, they saw Him. Two hundred and sixty of them describe it the same way and none of them can say what they mean by it. Skywalker did not glow, and nobody claims that he did. What they say is that they knew where he was, at any distance, even through rock and rubble. The closer they got to him the more the pressure eased. Following him was the only navigation anyone had for three days. He walked them to the shuttles. Then he went back and did it again. The Jedi accounts are the ones that unsettle the Order.

Five Jedi survived. All five have said, separately and in almost the same words, that what they felt was not a Jedi. A Jedi in the Force is a light. It pushes at the dark and the dark pushes back. You can feel the effort of it. You can feel it costing something. You can feel when the light is snuffed out.

Skywalker was not pushing at anything. What they describe is emptiness. Not absence in the sense of a hole in a wall, but a space so completely serene that the darkness could not reach into it. It had no edges to press on and nothing inside to work against. He was not winning, because he was not even fighting. He was simply a space the Sith nexus could not reach, and as he moved, the people followed him out. Kenobi has been asked about this eleven times and has answered the same. He said he had known Anakin Skywalker for thirty-two years and had never felt anything remotely like it. In private conversations that were never recorded, he said one thing: that whatever Anakin was supposed to become, everyone has just witnessed it.

### The rest of it

*Main article: [[The Battle of Yavin]]*

Anakin got people out constantly, seemingly without partaking in any food or sleep himself. He was still getting people out on the sixth day, inside the fire zone, after the shelling order had been given and while it was being carried out. He stopped a bombardment volley mid-flight and held it still for thirty minutes.

Of the sixteen hundred who came off that moon, six hundred escaped because of him. What the Republic will never publicly admit is that he helped the Collective as well. Around a hundred of them were saved, civilians and security who were only meant to protect the dig. It is the one fact out of Yavin that never reached the public. Senator Vess does not know it. If she did, the Republic would have no case left at all. Anakin met Kenobi on the third day. They spoke for about a minute and hugged. Neither has ever said what they talked about. Luke Skywalker was in the northern landing zone, and did not find his father there.

### The Path of Light

*Main article: [[The Path of Light]]*

It appeared in 1 ABY, about eight months after the last survivors came home. They call themselves the Path of Light. Individually they are Walkers. The Republic files them as the Cult of Light and has listed them as a terrorist organisation and a murder cult, both of which are partially accurate.

Their belief is straightforward: the prophecy is real. Skywalker is the Chosen One. What happened on Yavin 4 was a demonstration. He walked into the worst thing in the galaxy and it could not touch him. He brought six hundred people out of it on his own. He did this without belonging to any order or explaining himself to anyone. The Path holds that this is the path, that it is walkable, and that the way to walk it is to stop deciding and to act. They have killed at least sixty Republic officials in two years. The number is uncertain because the method varies. The Path claims nothing and does not seek recognition or glory.

Two things about them frighten the Ministry. The first is that they cannot be found. They are not Force-users. Four Walkers have ever been captured alive and all four tested flat. There is no corruption to detect, no Force signature. Templar investigators have walked through three fresh sites and reported an ordinary run of a room where somebody has died. Whatever the Path is doing, it is not being done by using the Force.

The second is the victims. Of the sixty-odd killings, investigators have gone on to establish that roughly seven in ten of the dead were taking money, running something, or covering for someone who was. This is not a characterisation by the Path's sympathisers. It is the Ministry's own number, produced by its own inquiries, most of which were only opened because there was a body of someone important.

Three in ten victims were clean. Those three are why the Path is hunted. The seven are why the hunting goes badly. A working sector ranger in the Rim now has to decide how hard to look for people who keep removing their own problems. Skywalker's own accuracy, across forty-one appearances, is nearer to a hundred percent. Nobody has been able to ask him what he thinks of the Path. Two Walkers have been recorded saying that he does not need to approve of them. Approval, they said, is a thing that people who choose require.

## XV. The Galaxy at 2 ABY

Twenty-one years after Chancellor Palpatine's death, the Low Republic is a bloated system strangling itself with its own rules, at war with the largest criminal organisation the galaxy has ever seen, which has just acquired a government, a flag and a lawyer.

Its charter is genuinely good and has been maintained lawfully, for two years running. Every renewal of emergency powers was done under a correct procedure. The rules have worked exactly as written and have delivered a warmonger as the Republic's Chancellor. Its Senate is free of the great corporations and full of their money. Its army is recruited, expensive and professional. Its intelligence service exposed the most powerful man in the Republic and is widely believed to have indirectly caused the disaster that followed. Two sector blocs, a total of eleven individual sectors, have walked out in two years. It contested neither, because it cannot. Sixty more sectors are currently trying to get out, their petitions pending.

The Jedi are two orders now, and each one says the other is a faction of itself. Over twenty-one hundred of them are back in the Coruscant Temple, drilling for war and expanding their numbers desperately, under Luminara Unduli who will tell anyone who asks that she is doing this because of her own student. Twenty-nine hundred are on Ossus, treating the wounded of both sides and refusing to pick sides. Somewhere between the two is a fifty-nine-year-old Kenobi who belongs to neither. He commands soldiers for a government he does not trust, but knows that someone has to do it.

Six hundred more Force users sit behind a four-thousand-year-old veil. They left the whole argument a decade ago and are healing and protecting worlds neither side of the war deems worthy of their attention.

In the south of Mandalore, an army clad in a thousand-year-old beskar is training to kill Force-users. It trains in ruins Bo-Katan Kryze gave up so that three quarters of her people could live without fearing for their lives.

In the shipyards of Eriadu there are at least ten new drydocks, each of them nineteen kilometres long. The Union has not stated what they are for.

A fifty-six-year-old Maul amasses power on Dathomir, when he is there at all. He presides over six hundred people who have stopped needing him. His order was founded on a promise that none of its members would ever be a tool. It is run day to day by Merrin who merely wants her people to be allowed to exist, and by Barriss Offee who has been making the same argument towards the Jedi Order since she was nineteen. He has told neither of them what he was looking for at Yavin. Forty kilometres away in the eastern highlands, Asajj Ventress holds the old rite, attempts a second shot at a peaceful life and diligently avoids contact with Quinlan Vos for reasons only she knows. Maul cares for none of that, his own paranoia causing him to be more distant and reckless with each passing month. Luke Skywalker went rogue and is currently on Maul's trail.

And somewhere across all of it walks a forty-three-year-old Anakin on no schedule, answerable to nothing, armed and entirely certain that he is being guided by a will far beyond himself. He has not decided anything in sixteen years and he keeps arriving where it matters. Closing in on him, following a trail that does not run in any direction, is the woman he trained.

Palpatine has been dead twenty-one years. Roughly a third of the galaxy still holds that the Jedi murdered a Chancellor and invented a Sith Lord afterwards to explain it. That view is not treated as fringe. It has senators, and it has had a great many more of them since Yavin.

The galaxy is bleak, on the edge of a precipice. Everyone has an idea on how to save it, but nobody can agree on what that is. This is the Low Republic, its fate is unwound, and it needs heroes more than ever.
