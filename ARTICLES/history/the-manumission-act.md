The **Manumission Act** of 16 BBY gave citizenship to all eight million living [[Clones|clones]], a quarter of them still children. It was drafted by [[Padmé Amidala]] and steered through by [[Bail Organa]], and it passed out of need as much as principle.

## The Reconstitution, 18–12 BBY

The chips came out between 18 and 17 BBY and every surviving trooper was cleared. Production was stopped in 17 BBY and the Kaminoan contract was cancelled the same year.

The Manumission Act of 16 BBY made every clone a citizen. [[Padmé Amidala]] drafted it and [[Bail Organa]] carried it. [[Ahsoka Tano]] and Captain [[Rex]] argued it through the Senate, giving more than four hundred hours of testimony between them. It passed as much out of necessity as conscience. Eight million living beings could not be released from service into nothing at all, with no standing under any law. Two million of them were children.

About half of the adults signed on again as volunteers. They served in the [[The Republic Corps#The Auxiliary Corps|Auxiliary Corps]], beside conscripted citizens who had been given a few weeks of training. The clones fought the rest of the [[The Long War|Long War]] as citizens of a Republic they had already defended for seven years without belonging to it. Most of them died before it ended. A little over three million adult clones were left alive when the Long War was over.

Beside them the Republic began raising a recruited army in 18 BBY, the first since the Ruusan Reformations. It was called the [[The Republic Corps|Republic Corps]]. The last Auxiliary regiment stood down in 12 BBY.

## The ones still growing

Production of clone batches stopped in 17 BBY, but a clone takes ten years to reach deployment age. Everyone decanted after 27 BBY was still growing when the contract was cancelled. There were about two million of them, and the youngest were only days old.

The Republic became their guardian overnight and had no plan for it. Although the main production contract was cancelled, the Kaminoans were kept on under a stripped-down agreement. Accusations and allegations about the immorality of their cloning practices were quietly dropped, in exchange for the Kaminoans taking on the duty to see the underaged clones to adulthood, because they already had the facilities and know-how to raise them. The work was done at cost and without enthusiasm on either side.

A Ministry of the Interior programme advertised the children for adoption across the Core and the Inner Rim. It ran for nine years, and out of the pool of two million, sixty thousand were adopted by independent families, fifty thousand were adopted by adult clone troopers, and three thousand were adopted directly by the [[The Reckoning of the Jedi|Jedi Order]] when they moved. Some of the latter decided to establish the [[Ossus Order#Temple Troopers|Temple Trooper]] regiment and still serve on Ossus.

Of course, this entire clone cohort was not a group with consistent age. The oldest underwent cadet training before production was stopped, so they were soldiers who were never deployed. Those decanted after 21 BBY were raised as [[Clones#The wards|wards]] and have never received accelerated combat training. All of them share a face and nothing else, aside from the distaste towards the Republic. There were also clone embryos still in the tanks in 17 BBY, but the Republic has never published what happened to them.

## The abandonment

The clones had grown fast and they aged fast to match. Troopers decanted in 32 BBY were physically thirty-six by 14 BBY, and fifty-six in 4 BBY. Citizenship arrived about a decade too late to be much use to the men who had won the war without it.

Their pensions had been worked out on the assumption that they would age like ordinary men. They were not corrected until 6 BBY, by which point most of the men they were meant for were dead. Laws to protect clone veterans looking for work were debated in four sessions running and passed in none of them.

The Manumission Act put about six million men out of service in a single year, even if half of them re-enlisted. Those that survived the Long War were dismissed gradually as their formations stood down, until the last in 12 BBY. Every one of them was physically old and legally brand new. They were trained for one job that no longer existed, distrusted by most of the galaxy, and drawing a pension calculated for a man of thirty. They were the largest pool of unattached military skill in the galaxy, and somebody was going to hire them.

Around one and a half million of the eight million are still alive in 2 ABY. Most of them never fought. The men who served were those decanted before 27 BBY and most are physically approaching seventy. The ones raised as wards are physically between forty and sixty. The latter are the reason the survivor numbers are as high as they are. Eighty-six thousand clones are still on [[The Shadow Collective|Collective]] payroll, which is where the medical care is.

After the pension correction passed in 6 BBY, to account for clone ageing, the clones that were still alive at least got enough funds to settle down and live out the rest of their days on some humble planet in the Mid or Outer Rim. [[Clones#Ko Sai's procedure|Slowing clone ageing]] has been possible for years but the Republic has never sponsored it. A man who ages twice as fast draws a pension for half as long.
