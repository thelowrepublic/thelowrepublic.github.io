**The Long War** was fought against the Confederacy of Independent Systems from 19 to 15 BBY. It closed with the Concord of [[Raxus]], a negotiated settlement in which nobody surrendered.

## Why it continued

The Confederacy of Independent Systems was not defeated in 19 BBY. It was beheaded. Dooku was dead. Grievous was killed on [[Utapau]] within hours of [[The Death of Palpatine|Palpatine]]. But the Separatist Council survived on [[Mustafar]]. The droid armies were still taking orders from regional commanders. The Confederacy still held some fifteen hundred member systems, along with the factories of the Techno Union, the Trade Federation, the InterGalactic Banking Clan, the Commerce Guild and the Corporate Alliance.

Nobody had the standing to surrender on its behalf. So the Separatists did not surrender. They broke into two dozen regional commands and fought on for four more years. The Long War was fought hardest in the [[Galactic regions#The Mid Rim|Mid Rim]]. The Taldot sector lost most of its industry and fourteen of its worlds were left completely uninhabitable. A war against two dozen enemies proved considerably more draining to finish than a war against one.

The Clone Wars have ended, and the Long War has begun. It was called as such not because it was much longer than the Clone Wars. Rather, it came directly after the previous conflict, with no break in between, making the galactic populace feel the length of the war much more profoundly.

## The double revelation

Proof that Dooku had served Sidious hit the Confederacy in two directions at once. It knocked the legs out from under the whole war. The Confederacy's founder had been the servant of the Chancellor he claimed to oppose. Separatist morale collapsed across hundreds of worlds within a year.

It also proved the Confederacy's original complaint far more thoroughly than anyone in the Confederacy had ever dared claim. The Republic had not merely been corrupt and bought by the great corporations. It had been run by a Sith Lord for thirteen years and nobody in it had noticed.

What this meant in practice was that the partisan fronts wanted to go home and their leaders could not. Peace meant trials and seized property for every executive who had signed a Confederate charter. The war's final years were fought largely by corporate boards with private armies. They were the only people left with nothing at all to gain from stopping.

## The Concord of Raxus, 15 BBY

The war ended in a bargain rather than a surrender. [[Lux Bonteri]] led the negotiations, because he was the only person there willing to acknowledge Separatist misgivings as legitimate. His mother had been murdered for arguing for exactly this.

Under the Concord, systems coming back into the Republic kept the right to run their own affairs. The great corporations lost their Senate seats for good. Soldiers below command rank were pardoned. The Confederate leadership went on trial. And the droid armies were to be broken up and scrapped, with the Republic overseeing the work.

The promises of self-government were kept, but taxes were still collected. The scrapping was never thoroughly finished. Nute Gunray and Rune Haako were tried and convicted in 14 BBY. Shu Mai vanished in 18 BBY with a great deal of the Commerce Guild's ready money and was never found.

## What a recruited army changed

*Main article: [[The Republic Corps]]*

The Senate began raising the Republic Corps in 18 BBY, within weeks of the [[The Kamino Findings|Kamino findings]]. The work took two years. The Corps was not ready in time for most of the Long War, so the war was finished by the [[The Republic Corps#The Auxiliary Corps|Auxiliary Corps]]. Its regiments were filled with clones who had signed on again and conscripted citizens given only a few weeks of training. They are the ones that took most of the war's last casualties. They were disbanded between 15 and 12 BBY, though there was not much left to disband.

A permanent army under civilian control created something that had not existed in 22 BBY: a permanent military interest. Shipyards with contracts. Veterans' associations. Garrisons in vital sectors and along trade lanes. A general staff with opinions about policy. All of it proved lasting.

More importantly, the dead came home. Clone casualties had been a line in a budget. Recruited casualties were somebody's constituents. They went back to their home worlds in numbered caskets and were mourned by voters who had a senator.

This is the main reason the Long War ended when it did. The Separatist remnants were not beaten. By 15 BBY the voters would no longer pay to beat them. The last holdout commands were bought off, bribed, or quietly given the self-rule they had spent seven years fighting for.

## The ascent

*Main article: [[Wilhuff Tarkin]]*

The Long War was [[Wilhuff Tarkin]]'s ladder. By 17 BBY he held the Outland Command. It was a theatre posting in the Rim with unusually wide freedom over supply, over who answered to whom, and over what happened to captured Separatist property. He used it to build something no Republic officer had held in a thousand years: a personal following in the officer corps, and a group of sectors that looked to him before they looked to Coruscant.
