After the death of [[The Death of Palpatine|Palpatine]] in 19 BBY, **the Republic was remade**. Five administrations have held office since. The Constitutional Convention of 13 to 11 BBY gave the [[The Galactic Republic|Republic]] a new charter with rules that had teeth. It also left a great deal unrepaired.

## Administrations

- 19 BBY (six weeks): [[Mas Amedda]], acting
- 19–14 BBY: [[Bail Organa]]
- 14–11 BBY: Ars Vehn
- 11 BBY – 0 ABY: [[Mon Mothma]]
- 0 ABY – present: [[Sevrin Dal]]

Organa governed through the war's continuation, the [[The Long War|trials]] and the [[The Kamino Findings|Kamino findings]]. He stepped down exhausted, having pleased nobody. Vehn, a former sector admiral, won on public exhaustion and governed as the Security faction's man. Mon Mothma was the first Chancellor to serve under the new charter. Dal was elected six weeks after [[The Battle of Yavin|Yavin]] on an open promise of revenge.

A Chancellor can be removed by the Senate on a two-thirds vote. Vehn is the only one it has ever managed. Three motions have been filed against Dal in two years. The best of them reached forty-one percent. All three came from the [[Three Stances of the Senate#The Rim self-rule bloc|Rim self-rule bloc]], and all three raised the number of sectors thinking about leaving.

## The Constitutional Convention, 13–11 BBY

It met under a Security government, which is the main reason it produced rules with teeth instead of a statement of good intentions.

[[Padmé Amidala]] chaired it. Through it all, she had to fight from the senatorial seat of [[Naboo]], a place that has been more scorned by the day, a place that produced Palpatine and [[Jar Jar Binks|Binks]]. A Sith Lord who nearly destroyed the Republic and a fool who made it possible due to his own idiocy. Add to it the inquiry into her husband's conduct at [[Anakin Skywalker#Murkhana|Murkhana]], and all this has put the Chancellorship permanently out of her reach. So she ended up writing the rules that other people would govern under.

What it achieved: emergency powers now expired automatically after ninety days unless the Senate voted to keep them, with no exceptions written in for any reason. Corporations were barred from holding Senate seats. The army and navy became permanent and answered to a civilian ministry rather than to the Chancellor. The courts were put out of the Chancellor's reach. And a standing [[The Galactic Republic#The Investigative Office|Investigative Office]] was set up with the power to demand any record the government held.

What it failed at: giving the [[Galactic regions#The Outer Rim|Outer Rim]] more seats, which the Core worlds voted down; getting rid of appointed sector governors; freeing the Jedi from the ordinary courts; and writing any law against slavery that could actually be enforced in or near [[Galactic regions#Hutt Space|Hutt space]].

The Convention also chose to say nothing whatsoever about how a sector might leave the Republic. The argument that won was that writing down a way out is an invitation to use it. So nothing was written down, keeping the status quo of no legal way to secede from the Republic. This turned out to prevent nothing.

## The Geonosis Programme

Superweapon plans of [[Geonosis|Geonosian]] design, recovered during the occupation in 18 BBY, became a secret programme under the Ministry of Security, paid for out of a budget line marked planetary defence. The new Investigative Office exposed it in 11 BBY at roughly eleven percent built. Vehn was immediately impeached. The programme's director, [[Orson Krennic]], was demoted and reassigned to Outland Command. The hull was scrapped. The plans and some Geonosian-made components were never recovered.

## What was not repaired

The Trade Federation lost its Senate seats and reappeared within four years as three separate companies with the same men on the board. Corruption in the sectors carried on. The courts were slower in 1 BBY than they had been in 19 BBY. The Outer Rim was still short of seats and short of policing, and still right in believing that the Core never thought about it. And then there were the droid papers.

### The droid papers

Between 21 and 15 BBY at least nine independent studies concluded that many Separatist droids displayed sentience and in some cases came out of the factories with faculties that bordered on self-awareness. Four of the nine studies were funded by Republic universities, and each one was buried. Two researchers lost their funding within a month. One was prosecuted for mishandling classified material, and the charge was quietly dropped after the war.

The Investigative Office published the lot in 6 BBY, together with the memoranda showing who had ordered them suppressed. The reasoning in those memoranda was not complicated. The Republic was about to destroy tens of millions of those things and did not want a debate about it first. Nothing has been done with this. There is no committee, no proposal and no law. The question of what exactly was scrapped under the [[The Long War#The Concord of Raxus, 15 BBY|Concord of Raxus]] is regarded on [[Coruscant]] as settled, in the sense that nobody will raise it.

## The census, 4 BBY

*Main article: [[The Census of 4 BBY]]*

The Convention ordered the first full census of the Republic in four centuries. With a galactic scale to work with, it took six years to finish. The Ministry of the Interior counted a little over eighty billion people in over a thousand sectors. To measure population change, the census needed older records. The Republic had none that reached back more than a few centuries, but the Jedi did. For over a thousand years the [[The Reckoning of the Jedi|Order]] had blood-tested newborns on member worlds, looking for Force-sensitive children, and it had kept every result. A thousand years ago, during the Republic Dark Age, the Candorian plague ravaged the galaxy. It killed two-thirds of the entire galactic population. Those who survived had fewer children, and so did their descendants. What the census also found is that the population decline had stopped.

The report was published in 4 BBY, and it caused quite a stir, though not anything close to the outrage that the [[The Kamino Findings|Kamino findings]] provoked. Small families had never been thought of as a condition; it was simply how things were. Now it had a beginning, and an end in sight.
