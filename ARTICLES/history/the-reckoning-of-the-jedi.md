**The Reckoning of the Jedi** was the eleven years after [[The Death of Palpatine|Palpatine's death]], from 19 to 8 BBY. In them [[Mace Windu|Windu]] was tried, a Sith shrine was found beneath the Jedi Temple, and the Jedi Order gave up its military rank, left [[Coruscant]] for [[Ossus]] and divided over attachment. The Ossus Compact of 8 BBY settled the schism only partly. Five hundred Jedi resigned after the shrine was found, and six hundred and thirty-two after the Compact.

## The Windu trial, 19–17 BBY

Two years, and the most closely watched trial in Republic history. [[Mace Windu|Windu]] was acquitted on the grounds that the threat had been immediate. The defence rested on three dead Jedi, the evidence from [[The Death of Palpatine|Palpatine]]'s chambers, [[Anakin Skywalker|Skywalker]]'s testimony, and in the final year the [[The Kamino Findings|Kamino findings]]. The verdict was sound and changed few minds. What lasted was the precedent, and Windu had set it himself on [[The Death of Palpatine#The first weeks|the first morning]] without meaning to. He resigned from the Council in 17 BBY and became a teacher and instructor at [[Ossus]] after the Order moved there.

## The shrine, 18 BBY

Digging beneath the [[Coruscant#The Jedi Temple|Jedi Temple]] turned up an ancient Sith shrine over which the Temple had been deliberately built. The Council chamber stood directly above it. The Order had a little over ten thousand members when the Clone Wars began, and close to two thousand of them died in the two wars. Five hundred Jedi resigned within eighteen months. The question that drove them out was not why the Order had failed to spot [[The Death of Palpatine|Sidious]]; that was already obvious. It was what the Order had been meditating on top of for a thousand years.

## Demilitarisation, 16 BBY

Jedi military rank was abolished by agreement between the Council and the Senate. In practice this followed from the [[The Manumission Act#The Reconstitution, 18–12 BBY|Reconstitution]] rather than from Jedi faults. [[Clones]] were being dismissed, and the new [[The Republic Corps|recruited force]] had no place in it for generals who took orders from a religious cult. Some of the Jedi that left the Order joined the army again as civilians. The Order gave up command in the middle of a war it had been fighting for six years. Several thousand Jedi were called home from places where they had spent those years defending particular people who had come to rely on them. They had to explain to those people why they were leaving. A good many never forgave the Council for it. The resentment left behind by that one administrative decision did the Order more damage over the following two decades than the Sith conspiracy had.

## The move to Ossus, 15 BBY

The Temple on [[Coruscant]] had been an excavation since 18 BBY. Investigators had been through every level of it. The shrine underneath had been opened, catalogued, photographed and argued about in the Senate. The Jedi Archives were turned upside down for any trace of Sith manipulation. The Council voted to leave in 15 BBY and the move took two years. The Order went to [[Ossus]]. It had been a Jedi world when the Republic was young, it held the ruins of the old library, and it had the considerable advantage of belonging to nobody. The Temple was left standing empty under Republic caretakership, and it stayed empty for thirteen years.

As the Jedi left Coruscant, they left an open invitation for all clone veterans to join them. Several thousand clones went immediately, and it's estimated that over the years Ossus has been a place where over eighty thousand [[Ossus#The clone settlements|clones lived out the rest of their days]]. By 2 ABY, around twenty thousand are still alive, albeit most elderly. Some started families, as several frontier villages sprang up on Ossus, at a respectful distance from the Jedi Temple. [[Shaak Ti]] is a liaison between the Temple and clone communities in those settlements, personally overseeing the wellbeing of remaining clones and doing her best to prolong their time with Force rituals. She has seen moderate success at it, though she did determine that children of clones are perfectly healthy and do not suffer from accelerated ageing or other health complications.

## The survivors

Because [[The Kamino Findings#The contingency orders|Order 66]] was never sent, the Order kept a generation of senior masters who would have otherwise died within an hour of one another. [[Plo Koon]], [[Ki-Adi-Mundi]], [[Aayla Secura]], [[Shaak Ti]], [[Luminara Unduli]], [[Stass Allie]] and [[Ossus Order|Coleman Kcaj]] all came through the [[The Long War|Long War]]. The preserved knowledge and continuity of leadership made the Order's restructuring smoother than it could've otherwise been. None of it prevented what followed.

## The schism, 14–8 BBY

The [[Anakin Skywalker|Skywalker]] marriage became public in 19 BBY, [[Padmé Amidala|Amidala]] having been visibly pregnant all through her husband's testimony. The Council refused to make an exception. He left the Order in 17 BBY. He was then immediately re-hired as a civilian and made general in the new [[The Republic Corps|Republic Corps]]. He was still one of the most effective and talented commanders in the Core and was more than willing to continue the fight.

The wider question could not be dealt with so easily, and three camps emerged. The reformists held that forbidding attachment had produced [[The Long War|Dooku]] and had very nearly corrupted [[Anakin Skywalker|Skywalker]]. Bonds you forbid do not stop existing, they argued. They simply stop being supervised. The traditionalists held that Skywalker proved the rule rather than broke it, and that the real mistake had been years of treating him like a favourite student and looking the other way for his misbehaviours. The withdrawalists held that both camps were treating symptoms. The Order's true error had been to serve a government at all, to create rules and regulations on how to feel and what to think, instead of rejecting ephemeral structures and following only the will of the Force.

[[Yoda]] moved towards the reformists over five years. His argument was that the Order's rigidity had been a worse failure than any particular teaching.

The Ossus Compact of 8 BBY settled matters partly, between the first two camps. Younglings could keep contact with their birth families. Openly acknowledged bonds were permitted. Possessive attachment was still forbidden. Where exactly the line fell was left to be judged case by case, which meant it was left to whoever happened to be judging.

The withdrawalists lost outright and did not argue further. Six hundred and thirty-two Jedi resigned in the eleven weeks after the Compact. They gave up their rank, their access, and their lightsabers, and scattered without saying where they were going. Among them was Master [[Aayla Secura]]. She had spent six years attached to the men she commanded and had been ordered to leave them. She came out of that decision agreeing with every word the withdrawalists said. A quarter of all the Jedi that survived the war left gradually over the whole reform period, not including the withdrawalist separation. Some became teachers, farmers and freight pilots. Some eventually found their way to [[Dathomir]]. The withdrawalists went [[Order of Voss#The withdrawalists|somewhere else entirely]], and it was five years before anyone found out where.
