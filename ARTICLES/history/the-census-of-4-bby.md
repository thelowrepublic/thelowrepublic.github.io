**The census of 4 BBY** was the first full count of the [[The Galactic Republic|Republic]] in four centuries. It took six years and counted a little over eighty billion people. Read against the Jedi Order's blood-test records, it showed a thousand years of decline since the Candorian plague. It also showed that the decline had stopped.

## The census, 4 BBY

The [[The Republic Remade#The Constitutional Convention, 13–11 BBY|Constitutional Convention]] ordered the first full census of the Republic in four centuries. With a galactic scale to work with, it took six years to finish. The Ministry of the Interior counted a little over eighty billion people in over a thousand sectors. That figure surprised many, but was logical in the end.

To measure population change, the census needed older records. The Republic had none that reached back more than a few centuries, but the Jedi did. For over a thousand years the [[The Reckoning of the Jedi|Order]] had blood-tested newborns on member worlds, looking for Force-sensitive children, and it had kept every result. Investigators had already been through those archives in 18 BBY, looking for traces of Sith manipulation. Nobody had considered them as a count of births until the census office asked.

The records confirmed something that everyone already accepted as fact and never brought up in daily life. Most habitable worlds lay abandoned, the ruins littering them being the stuff of legends for centuries now. Families are small and children are difficult to conceive. A household with three children is the talk of the town. What the records have shown is how long that had been true.

A thousand years ago, during the Republic Dark Age, the Candorian plague ravaged the galaxy. It killed two-thirds of the entire galactic population. Those who survived had fewer children, and so did their descendants. Every generation since has been smaller than the one before. Before the plague, the same worlds that now house billions or lie barren used to hold hundreds of trillions of lives, with [[Coruscant]] alone supporting three trillion lives.

What the census also found is that the population decline had stopped. Sometime in the last few decades, each generation began to replace itself again. People of the Republic are still mostly old, so the dead will outnumber the newborn for another thirty years, but after that the population should hold steady, for the first time in a thousand years.

The report was published in 4 BBY, and it caused quite a stir, though not anything close to the outrage that the [[The Kamino Findings|Kamino findings]] provoked. Small families had never been thought of as a condition; it was simply how things were. Now it had a beginning, and an end in sight.

## What kind of war it is

*Main article: [[The Shadow War#What kind of war it is]]*

The galaxy is very large and almost entirely empty. Perhaps one star system in a hundred has a world anyone can live on, and most inhabited worlds hold a single town, a mine or a few farms. The census of 4 BBY counted about eighty billion people in the Republic, with everyone else in the charted galaxy coming to perhaps two billion more.

On ninety-five percent of worlds, nothing has ever happened that necessitated a soldier's intervention. The [[The Shadow War|Shadow War]] is fought over that meaningful five percent. When measured against the whole galaxy, every number in the war looks small, but when measured against the places that matter, it starts to make sense.
