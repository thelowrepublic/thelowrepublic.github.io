**The Unified Scientific Consortium Conference** was a month-long gathering of Republic and Techno Union scientists, held in the second month of 12 BBY. It set out a common language for sciences that the wars had pushed forward and split apart. Among much else, it gave the galaxy aether theory and the Technology Privatisation Paradox. The Consortium that organised it still exists, and almost nobody remembers it.

## The Unified Scientific Consortium, 15–12 BBY

The wars of 22 to 15 BBY did a great deal for science. Both sides poured money into weapons, shields, droids and medicine, and both got results. They did not share them. The Republic's universities, the Navy's laboratories and the Techno Union's design bureaux each worked in their own terms and from their own first principles. Each body of theory held together within its own walls. Set side by side, they often disagreed on what a word meant, and sometimes on how the galaxy worked.

After the [[The Long War#The Concord of Raxus, 15 BBY|Concord of Raxus]], the [[The Galactic Republic|Republic]] and the Techno Union agreed to pool their scientists as a gesture of goodwill. The great corporations had lost their Senate seats, but not their laboratories. Together they founded the Unified Scientific Consortium, to compare findings, settle on one set of terms and check each side's discoveries against the other's. Its first task was a conference to present the results to the galaxy at large. Getting that far took three years.

## The Conference, 12 BBY

The Conference opened on [[Coruscant]] in the second month of 12 BBY and lasted a month. It drew researchers from across the Republic and from the former Confederate worlds. It received far less attention than the [[The Republic Remade#The Constitutional Convention, 13–11 BBY|Constitutional Convention]], which was sitting at the same time.

Most of what it presented was patient work on terminology, read today only by specialists. Two of its topics outlived it.

## Aether theory

Everyone who worked in space already knew the principles. Engineers built around them, gunners aimed by them and freighter crews told stories about them. What was missing was agreement. The same effect went by a different name in every navy, shipyard and laboratory, and each name came with its own partial explanation. Most worked well enough within a single trade and contradicted one another outside it.

The Conference replaced them with one account, reviewed by both sides, which it called aether theory. It told spacers nothing they did not already rely on. It explained why they could.

### The medium

Realspace is filled with a medium called the 'luminiferous aether', usually just 'the aether'. It is a thin, frictionless fluid. It fills the vacuum between the stars and runs through the gaps between atoms. Matter itself is ordinary. The aether is something separate, which presses on matter and carries waves.

The aether is a superfluid. At ordinary speeds it gives no drag at all, and a ship in open space keeps moving once its engines stop. Near lightspeed, drag and turbulence rise sharply. The older theories had never explained how one medium could be stiff enough to carry light and yet offer no resistance. The superfluid model settled the matter.

### Light, sound and blasts

Light, radio, sound and shockwaves are all waves in the aether. In open space a blast reaches an observer at the speed of light, so the flash and the boom arrive together. Inside an atmosphere, air still carries ordinary sound at its usual slower speed.

An explosion in space pushes against the aether as an explosion on a planet pushes against the air. Fireballs billow and shockwaves travel outward, as every gunner had always seen them do.

### The lightspeed barrier

Lightspeed is the speed at which waves cross the aether. It is a property of the medium, not a limit on reality, and time passes the same for a fast ship as for a slow one. No ship can cruise faster than light in realspace. Sublight engines cannot even reach it, because the drag climbs too steeply as they near it.

Crossing lightspeed throws up a shock front, as crossing the speed of sound does in air. That shock is what opens the way into hyperspace, and only a hyperdrive can force the crossing. The ship then travels inside a pocket of realspace aether, which spacers had long called the simu-tunnel.

The Conference was careful to say where the theory stops. The aether belongs to realspace alone. Hyperspace lanes, mass shadows and interdiction are matters of hyperspace itself, and aether theory says nothing about them.

### Blaster bolts

A blaster's power cell excites a gas, usually Tibanna, into a bolt of plasma. That much had been understood for centuries. What had never been explained was why the bolt holds together in a vacuum. Aether theory's answer is that the plasma rides inside a vortex of aether, which keeps it whole. The bolt travels as a visible pulse, slow enough to follow by eye, and it carries momentum. That is why it can be deflected.

### Exposure to vacuum

The aether has a pressure of its own. Because it runs between atoms, it fills living bodies too, as water fills a sponge, and the pressure inside a body matches the pressure outside it. A being exposed to vacuum does not burst, boil or freeze on the spot.

That is not the same as surviving. There is no air to breathe. An unprotected person stays conscious for some tens of seconds and dies within a few minutes, and cold and radiation do their own harm. A rescue within a minute or two is survivable. The exceptions are beings adapted to dense atmospheres, Skakoans among them, and compartments kept far above normal pressure. For both, sudden decompression can still kill.

A hull breach remains deadly for everyone. The escaping air throws people and objects out with it, and the breathable air is gone.

### The Force

One paper at the Conference proposed that the Force might be the aether as organised by living things, and that those sensitive to it might be feeling and steering its ripples. The Conference did not adopt it. Nobody could test it, and the [[The Reckoning of the Jedi|Jedi Order]], then in the middle of its [[The Reckoning of the Jedi#The schism, 14–8 BBY|schism]], sent nobody to answer it. It remains a scholar's guess.

### Open questions

The aether may not be the same everywhere. Readings near the galactic core show no 'aether wind', which the Conference put down to the core dragging the aether along with it. Readings from the Rim did not always agree, and the Conference left the question open.

## The Technology Privatisation Paradox

The second topic was the one the Techno Union tried hardest to keep off the programme.

### The ceiling

The galaxy's history is full of lost technology. Time and again, a power has built something far beyond what anyone else could build, ruled with it, and fallen. Its secrets fell with it. Centuries later someone else built something similar, and lost it the same way.

The Conference's explanation was privatisation. The most advanced technology has always been owned. Those who held it kept the designs secret, because the secret was the source of their power. When they fell, under their own weight or to those who rose against them, nobody else knew how the machines worked. Each civilisation reached the same ceiling, and took the ladder down with it.

### The common baseline

Common technology runs the other way. The blueprints of the ordinary freighter, the standard hyperdrive and the workhorse droid are freely available and have been copied for thousands of years. They are crude beside what has been lost. They have also never been lost.

These designs barely improve in capacity, because more capacity takes an exponentially larger investment, and nobody spends that on a public blueprint. What improves is reliability. Millions of small optimisations, each made by somebody with a repair to finish and too few parts, have worn the common designs down to what they cannot do without. This is why advanced technology tends to be fragile, and the most common technology is the sturdiest there is.

### The Techno Union's objection

The Techno Union opposed presenting the paradox at all. Its commerce rested on owning technology that nobody else could copy, and it did not care to hear that this made its products fragile and its secrets temporary. It was eventually overruled, and the paradox was presented.

## Afterwards

The Consortium fizzled out without much fanfare. The scientists met, made their acquaintances, and went back to their own projects across the galaxy. No second conference was ever called.

## In 2 ABY

The Unified Scientific Consortium still exists on paper, somewhere in the Republic's records. Hardly anyone remembers it. Its terms have fared better. Aether theory is taught as plain fact, by people who have never heard of the Conference that settled it.
