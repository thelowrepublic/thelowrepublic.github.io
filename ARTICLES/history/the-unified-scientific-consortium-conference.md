**The Unified Scientific Consortium Conference** was a month-long gathering of Republic and Techno Union scientists, held in the second month of 12 BBY. It set out a common language for sciences that the wars had advanced and split apart. Among much else, it gave the galaxy aether theory and the Technology Privatisation Paradox. The Consortium that organised it still exists, but almost nobody remembers it.

## The Unified Scientific Consortium, 15–12 BBY

The wars of 22 to 15 BBY did a great deal for science. Both sides poured money into weapons, starships, droids and medicine. However, they did not share the results. The Republic's universities and the Techno Union's design bureaus each worked on their own terms and from their own principles. Each body of theories and equations made sense within itself, but when set side by side, they often disagreed on what a word meant, and sometimes even on how the galaxy worked.

After the [[The Long War#The Concord of Raxus, 15 BBY|Concord of Raxus]], the [[The Galactic Republic|Republic]] and the Techno Union agreed to pool together their scientists as a gesture of goodwill. The great corporations had lost their Senate seats, but not their laboratories. Together they founded the Unified Scientific Consortium, to compare findings, settle on a universal set of terms and check each side's discoveries against the other's. Its first task was to organise a conference to present the results to the galaxy at large, which took three years.

## The Conference, 12 BBY

The Conference opened on [[Coruscant]] in the second month of 12 BBY and lasted a month. It drew researchers from across the Republic and from the former Confederate worlds, but received far less attention than the [[The Republic Remade#The Constitutional Convention, 13–11 BBY|Constitutional Convention]], which was sitting at the same time. Most of what it presented was patient work on unifying terminology, read today only by specialists, but two of its topics are more commonly known.

## Aether theory

Everyone who worked in space already knew these principles. Engineers built around them and gunners aimed by them, but what was missing was common language. The same effect went by a different name in every university, shipyard and laboratory, and each came with its own partial explanation. Most worked well enough within a single trade or atmosphere, but contradicted one another in differing environments. The Conference replaced them with one theory, reviewed by both sides, which it called aether theory. It didn't tell spacers anything they did not already rely on, but explained the whys and hows.

### The medium

Realspace is filled with a medium called the 'luminiferous aether', usually just 'the aether'. It is a thin, frictionless superfluid, which fills the vacuum between the stars and runs through the gaps between atoms. Matter itself is ordinary. The aether is something separate, which presses on matter and carries waves.

The aether is a superfluid, which means that at ordinary speeds it gives no drag at all, and a ship in open space keeps moving once its engines stop. Near lightspeed, drag and turbulence rise sharply. The older theories had never explained how one medium could be stiff enough to carry light and yet offer no resistance, until the superfluid model settled the matter.

### Light, sound and blasts

Light, radio, sound and shockwaves are all waves in the aether. In open space a blast reaches an observer at the speed of light, so the flash and the boom arrive together. This differs from what happens inside an atmosphere, where air still carries ordinary sound at its usual slower speed. An explosion in space pushes against the aether as an explosion on a planet pushes against the air, so fireballs billow and shockwaves travel outward, as every gunner had always seen them do.

### The lightspeed barrier

Lightspeed is the speed at which waves cross the aether. It is a property of the medium, not a limit of reality, and time passes the same for a fast ship as for a slow one. No ship can cruise faster than light in realspace. Sublight engines cannot even reach it, because the drag climbs too steeply as they near it.

Crossing lightspeed throws up a shock front, as crossing the speed of sound does in air. That shock is what opens the way into hyperspace, and only a hyperdrive can force that crossing. The ship then travels inside a pocket of realspace aether, which spacers had long called the simu-tunnel. Importantly, the aether belongs to realspace alone. Hyperspace lanes, mass shadows and interdiction are properties of hyperspace itself, and that is a completely different field of study.

### Blaster bolts

A blaster's power cell excites a gas, usually Tibanna, into a bolt of plasma. That much had been understood for centuries, but it had never been explained why the bolt holds together in space. Aether theory's answer is that the plasma rides inside a vortex of aether, which keeps it whole. The bolt travels as a visible pulse, slow enough to follow by eye, and it carries momentum.

### Exposure to space

The aether has a pressure of its own. Because it runs between atoms, it fills living bodies too, as water fills a sponge, and the pressure inside a body matches the pressure outside it. A being exposed to aether does not burst, boil or freeze on the spot, like it would do when exposed to a vacuum.

Lack of instantaneous death is not the same as safety. An unprotected person dies within a few minutes due to lack of air, and cold and radiation do their own harm. However, a rescue within a minute or two is survivable. The exceptions are beings adapted to dense atmospheres, Skakoans among them, and compartments kept far above normal pressure. For both, sudden decompression can still kill. A hull breach remains deadly for everyone. The escaping air throws people and objects out with it, and the breathable air is gone.

### Open questions

The aether may not be the same everywhere. Readings near the galactic core show no 'aether wind', which the Conference put down to the core dragging the aether along with it. Readings from the Rim did not always agree and limited readings from Wild Space were completely inconsistent, so the Conference left the question open.

## The Technology Privatisation Paradox

The galaxy's history is full of lost technology. Time and again, a great galactic power has built something far beyond what anyone else could build, ruled with it, and then fallen along with its secrets. Centuries later someone else built something similar, and lost it the same way. The Conference's explanation was privatisation, as the most advanced technologies have historically been owned, trademarked and restricted from widespread access. Those who held them kept the designs secret, because the exclusivity of a secret was the source of their power. When they fell, under their own weight or to those who rose against them, nobody else knew how to recreate their machines.

Common technology runs the other way. The blueprints of the ordinary freighter, the standard hyperdrive and the astromech are freely available and have been copied for thousands of years. They are crude in comparison to what has been lost, but they remain constant and accessible. These designs barely improve in capacity, because more capacity takes an exponentially larger resource investment, and no organisation would spend that on a public blueprint. What improves instead is reliability. Millions of small optimisations, each made by somebody with a repair to finish and too few parts, have perfected the common designs down to what they cannot do without. This is why advanced technology tends to be fragile, and the most common technology is the sturdiest there is.

The Techno Union opposed presenting the paradox at all. Its commerce rested on owning technology that nobody else could copy, and it did not care to hear that this made its products fragile and its secrets temporary. It was eventually overruled, and the findings backed by historical data were presented.

## Afterwards

The Consortium fizzled out without much fanfare. The scientists met, made their acquaintances, and went back to their own projects across the galaxy. The Consortium went on holding yearly meetings, but with a collaborative environment came new factors that many were not comfortable with: mutual criticism, petitions, votes and, worst of all, an ethics committee. The number of people who showed up to the yearly meetings kept falling exponentially. By 8 BBY the meetings were suspended indefinitely, for lack of attendance.

## In 2 ABY

The Unified Scientific Consortium still exists on paper, somewhere in the Republic's records. Hardly anyone remembers it. Aether theory is still taught as plain fact in every university in the galaxy.
