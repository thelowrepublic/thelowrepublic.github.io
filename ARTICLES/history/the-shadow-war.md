The **Shadow War** is the war the [[The Galactic Republic|Republic]] has fought against the [[The Shadow Collective|Shadow Collective]] since [[The Battle of Yavin|Yavin]] in 0 ABY. The Senate never declared it, and it is not a war of fronts: it is fought by auditors, customs inspectors and sector rangers, everywhere at once, over the few worlds that matter. The one place that looks like a target, [[The Secessions#Quelii and Gordian Reach, 2 ABY|Quelii]], is not attacked, and the Senate has renewed the Chancellor's emergency powers eight times.

## What kind of war it is

The Senate never declared war and could not have. In 0 ABY there was no state to declare it against. The Shadow War is the only name anyone has for it. The name came from a news broadcast in the first weeks and moved into the Senate record within the year. By 2 ABY the Ministry uses it in its own paperwork. It is not a war of fronts, but there are battles, of course. Fifty-two engagements in two years would be recognised as such by Republic records. But that is not where the real conflict is at. It is not like the Clone Wars, where millions of bodies were crushed under billions of droids. The scale is smaller, and the methods are different.

The [[The Shadow Collective|Shadow Collective]] has no military targets to lose because it has no military assets to defend. No vital worlds. No fleet worth engaging. No depot that matters. What it has instead is presence. Every settled world is a source of money and recruits. A dock that skims cargo. A magistrate on retainer. A quartermaster selling parts out of a garrison store. None of that can be bombed. They latch onto independent infrastructure, embed themselves into it and then benefit from it. They want the Rim booming with commotion, industry and trade, and have even invested their own funds to facilitate it, with the bulk of the credits going into charting new hyperspace routes. They stopped thinking like criminals and started to think like an empire, and it's working.

So the fighting is done by auditors, customs inspectors and [[The Judicial Forces and the Sector Rangers|sector rangers]], with the occasional raid. It is done everywhere at once and it never stops. A citizen of the [[Galactic regions#The Core|Core]] can go a lifetime without seeing a battle and will pay Collective money every week without knowing it, in the price of shipping and the cost of a permit. This is why the war is unpopular in a way the Clone Wars never were. There is no line on a map that is moving. There is only the discovery, every few months, that something you relied on had already been seized.

The galaxy is very large and almost entirely empty. Perhaps one star system in a hundred has a world anyone can live on, and most inhabited worlds hold a single town, a mine or a few farms. The [[The Census of 4 BBY|census of 4 BBY]] counted about eighty billion people in the Republic, with everyone else in the charted galaxy coming to perhaps two billion more. On ninety-five percent of worlds, nothing has ever happened that necessitated a soldier's intervention. The Shadow War is fought over that meaningful five percent. When measured against the whole galaxy, every number in the war looks small, but when measured against the places that matter, it starts to make sense.

## Why Quelii cannot be attacked

[[The Secessions#Quelii and Gordian Reach, 2 ABY|Quelii]] is the one place that looks like a target. [[Dathomir]] is the closest thing the Collective has to a capital and everything the [[The Nightborn|Nightborn]] are is made there. Taking it would be expensive but it would not be impossible. If the Republic committed itself, it could probably do it. That is not what stops it. The Republic's strength was never its army or fleet. It was that a thousand sectors believed membership was safer than leaving. Nothing enforces that belief. There is no garrison in most of the Rim and there never has been. Order holds because the Republic has spent centuries being the benevolent authority that does not come for you.

The day it burns a sector that left peacefully, that stops being true everywhere at once. And the sectors watching will learn that asking is dangerous, let alone leaving. They will stop filing petitions and start quietly withholding levies and taxes, because that is what a sector does when it has decided the Core is unreliable. Some are already doing that. Every credit withheld leaves the Republic less able to answer the next threat. The weaker it looks, the more sectors conclude, correctly, that it will not come for them either. It ends in slow dissolution or in a war against its own members. Nobody in the Ministry has produced a model where it ends anywhere else. So the fleet sits at [[Yavin 4]] and Quelii is not attacked.

The Collective understands this as clearly as the Republic does. Inside Quelii's borders its conduct has been immaculate. No extortion, no seizures, no unexplained deaths. Fees paid, contracts honoured, disputes taken to Quelii courts. All of the Collective's troops in Quelii are employed under legally established mercenary companies, with pensions and veteran funds. [[Republic Intelligence]] has spent two years looking for one clean incident of Collective aggression against the Republic inside Quelii and has not produced one. Outside those borders the syndicate has done all of it, constantly, rampaging through thousands of systems.

## The Republic at war

Chancellor [[Sevrin Dal]] asked for emergency powers eight days after the last survivors came home from [[The Battle of Yavin|Yavin]]. The Senate granted them and has renewed them eight times. He is blatantly pushing for war against [[The Secessions#Quelii and Gordian Reach, 2 ABY|Quelii]], but has not managed to sway the Senate just yet.

The [[The Republic Remade#The Constitutional Convention, 13–11 BBY|ninety-day rule]] has worked exactly as written on every single occasion. [[Padmé Amidala]], who wrote it, has said that the rule works and the politics beat it. She has not proposed changing it, on the grounds that she cannot think of a change that would have made any difference.

## What each side fields

What the Republic has grown since Yavin is not the [[The Republic Corps|Corps]]. The [[The Judicial Forces and the Sector Rangers|Judicial Forces]] were revived in 1 ABY after forty years of neglect and the Sector Rangers were properly funded for the first time in a century. The Ministry now spends the most on intel, private detectives, bounty hunters and security funds.

Its anti-Force programmes are the strangest line in the budget, fuelled by the paranoia of the [[The Nightborn|Nightborn]] rising to prominence. Over a year and a great deal of money have gone into securing and cordoning off the planet [[Myrkr]] to gather ysalamiri, a species of lizards that push the Force out of a bubble around them, and roughly two hundred of those are currently alive off-world. Cortosis-weave gauntlets are standard issue to escort units that expect to run into Nightborn. They will short out a lightsaber for about eight seconds, which is long enough. Taozin nodules, which hide a wearer from Force detection, are prohibited to Republic personnel and are worn by perhaps a third of [[Republic Intelligence]]. Lastly, a lot of budget goes into finding and securing any Sith ruins, so that Nightborn don't get to them first.

The [[The Shadow Collective|Collective]]'s advantage is that almost everything it fields costs next to nothing. The [[The Long War#The Concord of Raxus, 15 BBY|Concord]] left something near four million droid hulls unaccounted for across thousands of systems. Around two hundred thousand have been recovered and returned to service. Three Lucrehulk hulls in the [[The Secessions#Quelii and Gordian Reach, 2 ABY|Gordian Reach]] have been stripped down for their foundries and communication hubs, bolstering the rate at which the Collective refurbishes droids. Cortosis-weave BXs were a Separatist experiment that never reached production and are now in production. Its best anti-Jedi asset is older than any of it: IG-100 MagnaGuards, built to [[The Long War|Grievous]]'s specification for killing Force-users, of which about nine hundred are operational. Then there are all the ancient Sith treasures that [[Maul]] dug up. Only a select few Nightborn know all the specifics, but they are known to be in possession of holocrons dating back to the ancient Sith Empire.

The difference in approach follows from the difference in cost. The Republic has to be everywhere and can only afford to pick targets. The Collective has no budget unless it actively extorts a population, so it fights only where it's already profitable to stay.
