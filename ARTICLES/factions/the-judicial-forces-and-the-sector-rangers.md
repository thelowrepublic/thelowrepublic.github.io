The **Judicial Forces** and the **Sector Rangers** are the [[The Galactic Republic|Republic]]'s officers of the law and its patrols, and it is they, rather than the [[The Republic Corps|Corps]], who carry its weight on the ground. Most of the fighting in the [[The Shadow War|Shadow War]] falls to them.

## Revival, 1 ABY

What the Republic has grown since [[The Battle of Yavin|Yavin]] is not the [[The Republic Corps|Corps]]. The Judicial Forces were revived in 1 ABY after forty years of neglect and the Sector Rangers were properly funded for the first time in a century. The Ministry now spends the most on intel, private detectives, bounty hunters and security funds.

## The two forces

The Republic's real strength on the ground is not the Corps, but the Judicial Forces and the Sector Rangers. The Judicial Forces serve the courts: they investigate, arrest and are entitled to pass judgement in local matters. The Sector Rangers patrol everything that the Corps doesn't: ports, roads, outlying settlements and the lanes between worlds. There are about one hundred million Sector Rangers, scattered across the galaxy, and about one million Judicial Forces investigators. Together they are more than twelve times what the Corps can muster.

## How they are run

The catch is that neither is an army. Both are generously funded from Coruscant, but run from the ground. Most of their officers were local police and militias before 1 ABY. The Republic did not recruit them like they did the Corps. All it needed to do was pay them, arm them and deputise them by putting the Republic's name on their uniforms. Local authorities and each sector's Republic representatives decide who serves and where, so that Coruscant doesn't need to keep their names in an already bloated record. The Senate does not know how many of them there are on any given world, and they do not care.

That is also why they cannot be moved. A Ranger hired on [[Ord Mantell]] works on Ord Mantell, patrols the same streets for twenty years and goes home at the end of every shift. No Chancellor can send that person to [[The Secessions#Quelii and Gordian Reach, 2 ABY|Quelii]].

## The central office

The Judicial Forces work under the Judicial Department, which answers to the Ministry of Security. Each sector has its own separate branch of the Department, which handles cases at the local level. Above the branches is the Judicial Oversight Office, a small central office led by chief criminal investigator [[Eogun Vidoc]]. It oversees the sector branches and handles the crimes that cross sector lines. It works alongside the central Judicial Department but does not answer to it, only to the Ministry of Security. Technically, that puts Vidoc in charge of all the Judicial Forces, but not of the Department itself, which is run by Republic bureaucrats.

Vidoc hunted bounties for twenty-two years first, and that is where he learned the law. He moves faster than the Ministry can authorise and has never broken a law in his life. The [[The Shadow Collective|Collective]]'s bounty on his head has seven digits.

## Commissioners

The Sector Rangers have no oversight office of their own. Every local branch falls directly under the Ministry of Security. The highest rank among the Rangers is Commissioner, who is responsible for a whole sector, or for a single planet if it is vital enough. A Commissioner is usually a Ministry of Security official, sent out to coordinate the sector and to work with the local [[The Republic Corps|Corps]] division, if there is one. A local officer rising to Commissioner is rare, but not unheard of, especially in sectors where loyalty to the Republic runs low. There, raising a local to the top post is a form of appeasement. One exception is on [[Ferrix]], where the post fell to [[Syril Karn]], a corporate security officer from Morlana One, when the Corps deputised the planet's whole security force.

## In 2 ABY

The [[The Shadow War|Shadow War]] itself is fought mostly by the Judicial Forces and the Sector Rangers. The [[The Republic Corps|Corps]] is called in when a raid needs numbers or a gang war escalates into an actual planetary war.

The Senate judges both organisations only by two numbers. One is how much money went into a sector, and the other is how fast its crime rate fell. Nothing else reaches Coruscant. Where the local authority is honest, the arrangement works very well. Where it is not, the Republic is unknowingly paying for the [[The Shadow Collective|Collective]]'s police, and the crime is still kept in check, just for a different reason.
