The **Corporate Security Alliance** is a standing security force paid for by the corporate boards of the Corporate Sector and its neighbours. They founded it in 2 ABY to drive the [[The Shadow Collective|Shadow Collective]] off their worlds, and it has done so wherever it has fought. How it raised its soldiers so quickly is a question the [[The Galactic Republic|Republic]] cannot answer.

## The corporations and the Collective

The corporations that govern the Corporate Sector had been losing ground to the Shadow Collective for years. [[The Shadow Collective#Crimson Dawn|Crimson Dawn]] bought their foremen and Black Sun bought their customs officers. Their own security forces were hired cheaply and could be bribed just as cheaply. Many of the boards had sat in the Senate until the [[The Long War#The Concord of Raxus, 15 BBY|Concord of Raxus]] took those seats away.

## Ferrix, 2 ABY

*Main article: [[Ferrix#Ferrix Massacre, 2 ABY]]*

[[Ferrix]] changed that. When [[The Secessions#Quelii and Gordian Reach, 2 ABY|Quelii]] seceded, the Republic suddenly needed a foothold on the Hydian Way, and by the third month of 2 ABY it had taken Ferrix back by force. No Collective asset on the planet survived, more than seven hundred civilians died with them, and the world was handed to a new set of administrators from the Corporate Sector. The Republic described the operation as a follow-up to requests for aid from years before.

The boards took note. Removing the Collective from a corporate world by force now had a precedent, and the Republic had set it. They also knew that the Republic would only do it again when it needed another world for itself.

## The agreement, 2 ABY

The Corporate Security Alliance was signed a month later, in the fourth month of 2 ABY. Its members are the corporate boards of the Corporate Sector and of the sectors around it, which the same interests govern, directly or indirectly. They agreed to pool their security budgets into a single standing force. The agreement calls it a 'peacekeeping force' and gives it one stated purpose: to remove the Collective from member worlds. The first ground forces deployed in the sixth month of 2 ABY.

The Alliance has broken no law. Corporate authorities have kept their own security forces for as long as there have been corporate authorities. The Concord that took the boards' Senate seats and broke up their droid armies said nothing about hired security force.

## Troopers

Alliance troopers fight well. They hold a line and follow orders to the letter, but they are not commandos. [[The Republic Corps|Republic Corps]] officers who have fought near them call them dependable while a plan holds and a mess once it breaks.

Their armour is a mass-produced copy of the old Phase II clone pattern, from blueprints the Alliance has certainly acquired through entirely legal channels. The corporate boards call what comes out of the factories 'economically optimised', which means cheaper materials and thinner plates. Rim dockworkers call the troopers 'stock', because shareholders paid for them.

## Operations

On the ground the Alliance has been effective. On every world it has re-taken the Collective has not returned yet, though the old corporate quotas have. Every operation in the Alliance's reports is an operation against the Collective, but it is the Alliance that decides what counts as the Collective. On two mining worlds, that has included a strike, classified as 'Collective agitation'.

## The Republic and the Alliance

For the Republic the Alliance is convenient. The [[The Republic Corps|Corps]] garrisons almost nothing in that part of the Rim, and the Alliance fights the Collective there at no cost to Coruscant. Chancellor [[Sevrin Dal]] has floated the idea of formally inviting the Alliance into the war. He has not put it to the Senate as a motion, since that would stretch the charter further than even he can wave away. The Ministry has been more careful. The last time corporate boards kept private armies, they got notions of independence, and the galaxy got the Clone Wars.

## The numbers

What troubles the Ministry most is not what the Alliance does but how fast it became able to do it. Its first troops were in the field, armed and armoured, two months after the signing. By the end of the year it had declared a force of fifty thousand at its shareholder meetings, with more supposedly on the way. The Corps took two years to stand up, and the [[The Grand Union Army#The armour|Union]] spent ten years readying its factories for its armour alone.

The Alliance says it hired its soldiers. Its recruiters supposedly took on mercenaries, [[Clones|clone]] veterans and anyone else willing to fight. That pool is well known to every intelligence service, and it is neither large nor easy to reach in such a short time. In the Outer Rim the Collective outbids everyone, which is why the Corps gets almost no recruits there. Most clone veterans still fit to fight joined the Collective years ago.

The numbers do not add up. So far, [[Republic Intelligence]] has given the Senate three explanations. Either the Alliance recruited faster than any army in history, or it began recruiting long before it signed anything, or it has found a way for soldiers to spring out of the ground like mushrooms after rain. The Alliance has never left its dead on a field and has never published a casualty list. No trooper has deserted, or if one has, they have never spoken to the press. If the Collective has taken any of them alive, it has not said so.

## In 2 ABY

[[Republic Intelligence]] opened a file on the Alliance the week it was signed. Corps marshals in the neighbouring sectors now report Alliance movements alongside Collective ones.
