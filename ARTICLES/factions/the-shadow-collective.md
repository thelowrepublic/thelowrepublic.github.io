The **Shadow Collective** is what [[Maul]] calls the whole of the organisation he leads, from the syndicates to the [[The Nightborn|Nightborn]]. It has been at war with the [[The Galactic Republic|Republic]] since 0 ABY, in what is called the [[The Shadow War|Shadow War]]. 

## What it is

The Shadow Collective is Maul's whole organisation and his own name for it. It takes in the criminal syndicates, meaning Black Sun, the trading arm that works under the name Crimson Dawn and formerly the Pyke Syndicate, now its cast-offs known as Shadow Pykes. Where Crimson Dawn is the trading arm, Black Sun is the murdering arm, and it holds most of the organisation's enforcers, assassins and ground troops who are not clones or droids. The Collective also takes in the [[Mandalore#The armouries|Mandalorian Death Watch]] under [[Gar Saxon]], salvaged droid formations, clone legions saved from ageing by black market procedures, hired ground troops, and the [[The Nightborn|Nightborn]].

The Nightborn are only one part of it: the order of Force-users based on [[Dathomir]]. They are not the Shadow Collective and they do not run it, though they are its most feared element and the part the Republic talks about most. The difference matters, because the Republic is at war with the Shadow Collective. It has never been at war with the Nightborn and has never outlawed them by name. In practice the distinction has mattered less than it sounds. Nearly every Nightborn the Republic has arrested was taken during a Collective operation. They are charged as criminals or as terrorists, like anyone else on the raid. The exceptions are few and they are the ones that matter. A small number were arrested for what they were and not for anything they had done. Those are the cases Senator [[Ilai Vess|Vess]] keeps track of.

## Crimson Dawn

[[Qi'ra]] runs Crimson Dawn since 6 BBY, the Shadow Collective's trading arm and its main source of money. She has met [[Maul]] in person on eleven recorded occasions and has outlasted three predecessors who met him more often than that.

No civilian employer would take [[Wolffe]] after his discharge. Crimson Dawn did, in 7 BBY. He became a recruiter. Over half the clone veterans on Collective payroll came in, directly or indirectly, through him. He arrives with a contract, a medical plan, a life extension treatment and a rank. And he is a fellow clone.

[[Han Solo]] flew for Crimson Dawn for six years under Qi'ra, whom he had known before either of them worked for anyone. He was good at it, paid well, and did not ask what was in the sealed crates. Crimson Dawn priced [[Hondo Ohnaka]] out of four routes in 3 BBY and he couldn't really fight it.

## The discarded

The Shadow Collective's strength is not exotic. Its strength is that it hires the people the Republic was finished with. The law no longer puts food on the table or gives your children a roof to sleep under. The Collective does.

Between 16 and 7 BBY the Republic turned more than five million [[Clones|clones]] out into the galaxy. They were new citizens on paper and more than half were elderly in the body, trained for a job that no longer existed, living on a pension worked out for a man in his thirties. The Senate debated protecting them four sessions in a row and passed nothing. Crimson Dawn offered contracts, quarters, rank, and medical care suited to men ageing at twice the ordinary rate. By the Republic's own cautious estimate, around ten percent took the deal and by 2 ABY around eighty-six thousand of them still work for the Collective.

A large number of Shadow Collective ground units are commanded by men the Republic grew, freed, and then could not find a use for. Many Collective soldiers are rigorously trained by those same men, and getting deadlier by the year. The Collective also sponsors [[Clones#Ko Sai's procedure|Ko Sai's procedure]] for all clones working for it. It is genome work followed by months of drug therapy to make the changes hold. It's done once, and after that every man takes a maintenance course of pills priced so low that nobody notices it. Naturally, the price is not the issue, but the availability is. Even if a clone purchases the procedure with independent funds, he will need to keep returning to the Collective for refills. For an old man, the procedure halves their accelerated ageing. If done when the clone is young enough, it returns him to ordinary ageing altogether.

The rest of the recruiting pool is simpler. Rim worlds that the [[The Long War#The Concord of Raxus, 15 BBY|Concord]] promised self-rule and the Senate then forgot to pay for. Separatist officers pardoned straight into unemployment. Mandalorians who have lost three civil wars. And the ordinary poor of countless systems, who are offered solid wages for work that carries a real chance of dying, which is more than the Republic offers them at all. Desperation brought most of them in. What has kept many of them is the discovery that, inside the Collective, they have the power to make sure the same desperation never reaches anyone else.

## The droid forces

The [[The Long War#The Concord of Raxus, 15 BBY|Concord of Raxus]] required the droid armies to be scrapped and the job was never finished. The Shadow Collective finds that hardware, repairs it and fields it. It is twenty years out of date, it is almost free, and there is a very great deal of it. Since the [[The Republic Remade#The droid papers|droid papers]] came out in 6 BBY there has also been an argument attached to it. The Republic scrapped them, despite the fact that some of them were self-aware beings. The Collective repairs them, gives them orders, and supposedly even gives some units rank. The practice might be performative, but does wonders for public perception.

The Collective's advantage is that almost everything it fields costs next to nothing. The Concord left something near four million droid hulls unaccounted for across thousands of systems. Three Lucrehulk hulls in the [[The Secessions#Quelii and Gordian Reach, 2 ABY|Gordian Reach]] have been stripped down for their foundries and communication hubs, bolstering the rate at which the Collective refurbishes droids. Cortosis-weave BXs were a Separatist experiment that never reached production and are now in production. Its best anti-Jedi asset is older than any of it: IG-100 MagnaGuards, built to [[The Long War|Grievous]]'s specification for killing Force-users, of which about nine hundred are operational.

[[Gizor Dellso]] is chief engineer of the Collective's droid programme. He has returned near two hundred thousand hulls to service in eleven years. He does not upgrade them. He says the design was perfect in 22 BBY and every droid army loss was a matter of quantity, not quality.

[[Patient Yield]] is a Lucrehulk control core. It has run droid refit for eleven years, files its production reports on schedule, and has three thousand organic workers under it. Gizor Dellso reports to it, contrary to all common sense.

[[Kalani]], a super tactical droid, was found in 4 BBY on Agamar along with a small army of fully functional battle droids. When questioned why his army was still operating, he claimed to have intercepted the shutdown signal that was sent out at the end of the Long War and prevented it from reaching any of the droids under his command. Recruiting him was as simple as treating him like the general he was. Now he holds that rank under the Collective and has commanded ground actions in sixteen systems. The Republic considers the rank a piece of theatre. Kalani has twice refused reckless orders on the grounds that they would have destroyed units under his command. Reports of his behaviour during the Clone Wars were the basis of four out of nine of the [[The Republic Remade#The droid papers|droid papers]] of 6 BBY.

## How the Collective thrives

The Collective has absorbed several hundred local syndicates and crime families since 12 BBY and has rarely turned one away. They remain what they were, criminals through and through. Every one of them runs protection, moves contraband and kills people who become inconvenient. What changed is the standard they are measured against. A syndicate that keeps a hospital open and a port running has outdone forty years of Republic rule.

There is no law underneath, only temperament. Some bosses behave like landlords who intend to keep their property nice and orderly. They repair what they use and pay for schools their own people attend. They still take far more out than they put back in, but that is more than the Republic ever gave. Others are warlords and rule accordingly, with a whip and an iron fist. A world's fortune depends entirely on which of them it fell to.

[[Maul]] does not regulate this and does not care to. Every one of them is a criminal, and the only thing that can be asked of a criminal is if the money is right. And it always is. [[Nadi Orl]] audits all of them quarterly and checks if the ledgers are in order. If anything is amiss, they are warned once and severely fined. If they fail again, they are eliminated and replaced.

## The Hutts

*Main article: [[The Hutt Clans]]*

The [[The Hutt Clans|Hutt Clans]] deal with the Shadow Collective constantly and have never once submitted to it. Jabba Desilijic Tiure has done business with Crimson Dawn for eighteen years. He defers to [[Maul]] in nothing and treats the whole relationship as trade between equals, which infuriates Maul and is probably the point.

[[Sarn Vekh]] took about a fifth of the [[The Hutt Clans#The Pykes|Pyke Syndicate]] and stayed loyal to Maul as the Shadow Pykes. The Syndicate calls them cast-offs. The Collective calls them turncoats and has never stopped watching them.

## Why it is winning the argument

The Republic is a system of rules that has spent twenty years learning to tie its own hands. Anything it attempts to do has to survive an investigating office, a ninety-day vote and four committees. Its charter works. That is the trouble. It works at the speed it was built to work at, and it was built by people whose worst fear was a government that could move too fast.

The Shadow Collective has no charter. It takes in whatever the Republic throws out. It changes its roster, bosses and bases week to week. It holds no ground it cannot walk away from and has never once had to explain itself to anybody. In a galaxy of law, orders and structures, that would be a weakness. In this one, chaos has been their strength for many years.

It has also become a banner. Where a governor is corrupt, a board is cruel or the Republic does not answer, the Collective is the only power that can push back, and people who would never have called themselves criminals join it for that reason alone. Inside it, the line between a gangster and a rebel has never been harder to draw.

## The Shadow War

*Main article: [[The Shadow War]]*

The Shadow Collective has no military targets to lose because it has no military assets to defend. No vital worlds. No fleet worth engaging. No depot that matters. What it has instead is presence. Every settled world is a source of money and recruits. A dock that skims cargo. A magistrate on retainer. A quartermaster selling parts out of a garrison store. None of that can be bombed. They latch onto independent infrastructure, embed themselves into it and then benefit from it. They want the Rim booming with commotion, industry and trade, and have even invested their own funds to facilitate it, with the bulk of the credits going into charting new hyperspace routes. They stopped thinking like criminals and started to think like an empire, and it's working.

The difference in approach follows from the difference in cost. The Republic has to be everywhere and can only afford to pick targets. The Collective has no budget unless it actively extorts a population, so it fights only where it's already profitable to stay.

The war has undone much of what the Collective was becoming. The Republic draws no line between a boss who keeps a hospital open and one who kills for hire, and its bounty list pays the same for both. The landlords now spend on guns what they used to spend on charity, and the warlords have found that a war makes them useful again.

### Quelii

The Collective understands why [[The Shadow War#Why Quelii cannot be attacked|Quelii is not attacked]] as clearly as the Republic does. Inside [[The Secessions#Quelii and Gordian Reach, 2 ABY|Quelii]]'s borders its conduct has been immaculate. No extortion, no seizures, no unexplained deaths. Fees paid, contracts honoured, disputes taken to Quelii courts. All of the Collective's troops in Quelii are employed under legally established mercenary companies, with pensions and veteran funds. Republic Intelligence has spent two years looking for one clean incident of Collective aggression against the Republic inside Quelii and has not produced one. Outside those borders the syndicate has done all of it, constantly, rampaging through thousands of systems.

## In 2 ABY

Twenty-one years after Chancellor [[The Death of Palpatine|Palpatine]]'s death, the Low Republic is a bloated system strangling itself with its own rules, at war with the largest criminal organisation the galaxy has ever seen, which has just acquired a government, a flag and a lawyer.

[[Nadi Orl]] is chief of accounts for the Shadow Collective. Thanks to her, the Collective pays on time in hundreds of systems, and over eighty thousand clones receive good pay, expert medical oversight, and retirement to comfortable lives when their contracts run out. [[Maul]] has never overruled her. [[Republic Intelligence]] rates her the highest-value target in the organisation.
